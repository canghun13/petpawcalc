Frequently Asked Questions

How many days of supplies should a pet emergency kit have?

A common minimum is 3 days of food, water, and medication, which covers most short-notice evacuations. Several disaster-preparedness organizations recommend closer to 7 days where possible, since extended disasters can delay a return home or access to pet supply stores longer than expected.

Do I need a separate emergency kit for each pet?

Documents, food, and medications should be individually labeled per pet since dosages and dietary needs differ, but shared items like a first aid kit, extra towels, or a manual can opener can be kept in one central kit. Each pet still needs their own carrier — trying to share a single carrier between multiple pets during a stressful evacuation rarely goes smoothly.

What documents should be in a pet emergency kit?

Vaccination records (especially rabies), a recent photo with you in the frame for proof of ownership if you're separated, microchip registration details, proof of ownership or adoption paperwork, and a written list of current medications and dosages. Keep physical copies in a waterproof bag or folder, since power and phone service aren't guaranteed during a disaster.

Should I include my pet's medications in the kit?

Yes, ideally a 7-14 day supply of any regular medication, stored in a waterproof container and rotated periodically so it doesn't expire unnoticed. If your pet takes a medication that requires refrigeration, plan how you'd keep it cool during an evacuation and mention this to your vet in advance.

What if I have to evacuate before my pet's kit is fully ready?

Always take your pet with you rather than leaving them behind to retrieve supplies later — animals left behind during a disaster are easily lost, injured, or unable to be rescued in time. A "pets inside" rescue alert sticker on a front window or door helps responders know to look for them if you're ever unable to evacuate together.

Do pet shelters or hotels always accept pets during a disaster?

No — many public emergency shelters do not allow pets inside, which is why researching pet-friendly hotels and pet-specific shelters along your likely evacuation routes ahead of time matters. Waiting until an evacuation order to search for one can mean arriving to find it already full or unavailable.
