Frequently Asked Questions

How do I suspend absences if I have a job?

By assembling coverage rather than by being home yourself — it does not have to be you. The usual pieces are a housemate or partner staggering their hours, a neighbour who is home during the day, doggy daycare, a dog walker or sitter for the middle of the day, working from home on some days, and taking the dog with you where that is possible. Most households end up combining several. It is a genuinely big ask and pretending otherwise helps nobody, but it is temporary, and it is the thing that decides whether months of training produce anything.

What if I miss a day and have to leave my dog?

It happens, and it is not a reason to abandon the plan — but it is a reason to go backwards deliberately rather than carrying on where you left off. A full panic episode undoes progress, so treat the next few sessions as shorter than before and rebuild. What matters is the pattern rather than any single day: occasional slips with a rebuild are survivable, while training three times a week and leaving for eight hours in between is a treadmill that goes nowhere.

Should I get a second dog to keep them company?

It is one of the most common suggestions and it frequently does not help. Separation anxiety in the strict sense is distress at being apart from a particular person, so another dog does not address it — and you may end up with two dogs to manage instead of one. It can help where the problem is isolation distress, meaning the dog is fine with any company at all. That distinction is worth establishing from video and a proper assessment before taking on a second animal for a reason that may not hold.

Will exercising my dog more fix it?

Not on its own. A well-exercised dog is easier to live with and exercise belongs in the plan, but anxiety-driven behaviour persists even when a dog's physical and mental needs are fully met — that persistence is one of the ways behaviourists separate anxiety from boredom. If a long walk before you leave genuinely resolves the problem, what you had was probably under-stimulation rather than panic, which is a much better situation to be in.

Do calming products, music or pheromones work?

They sit alongside training rather than replacing it. Pheromone diffusers, calming music, snug wraps and food-dispensing puzzle toys are all commonly suggested and may take a small edge off, but none of them teaches a dog that being alone is safe. Two cautions worth knowing: a stuffed toy is often only a brief distraction and can become a target for destruction, and camera devices that let you talk to your dog can make things worse for severe cases, because hearing you without being able to reach you is its own kind of distress.

When should I get professional help?

Sooner than most people do. A veterinary behaviourist or a trainer who specialises in separation anxiety is worth the money for moderate to severe cases, for dogs injuring themselves, and for anyone whose progress has stalled. Medication is a legitimate part of treatment for many dogs and often what makes training possible at all, and that is a veterinary decision. Separation anxiety is also one of the hardest problems to self-manage because the protocol demands precise observation and a willingness to go backwards — both things an outside pair of eyes makes considerably easier.
