Frequently Asked Questions

Why is antifreeze so dangerous for pets?

Ethylene glycol, the active ingredient in most antifreeze, tastes sweet, so dogs and cats will readily lap up a spill or drink from a puddle under a leaking car. Very small quantities can cause fatal kidney failure, and cats are affected by strikingly little. The other problem is timing: treatment is far more effective the sooner it starts, and by the time obvious symptoms appear the damage may already be done. Treat any suspected exposure as an immediate emergency call rather than something to watch and see.

Is pet-safe ice melt actually safe?

"Pet-safe" and "pet-friendly" are marketing terms rather than regulated ones, so they mean less than they suggest. Products based on urea or magnesium chloride are generally gentler on paws than rock salt, but most are still irritating if eaten in quantity, and your dog is walking on whatever your neighbours used regardless of what you buy. The dependable protection is mechanical: rinse and dry the paws after every walk on treated pavement, which removes the residue before your dog licks it off.

Should I shave my double-coated dog in winter?

No. A double coat insulates in both directions by trapping air, and shaving it removes the insulation your dog is relying on. Beyond winter warmth, shaving a double coat can permanently change how it regrows in some dogs. What the coat does need is brushing — a matted coat cannot trap air properly, so grooming is doing more for warmth in winter than any haircut would.

Do dogs need more food in winter?

It depends entirely on the dog, and the common assumption gets it backwards for most pets. A dog working or living outdoors in the cold genuinely burns more calories staying warm. A house dog whose walks have been cut from an hour to fifteen minutes is burning fewer, and winter weight gain is a real pattern in indoor pets. Judge by body condition every few weeks rather than by the season, and adjust portions in that direction.

Why should I bang on the car hood in winter?

Cats — both pets and community cats — climb into wheel wells and onto engine blocks that are still warm from a recent drive, because it is the warmest dry spot available. Starting the engine with a cat inside causes severe or fatal injuries. Banging on the hood and giving the car a moment before starting it takes seconds and is one of the highest-value winter habits there is, whether or not you own a cat yourself.

What are the first signs of hypothermia in pets?

Shivering comes first, along with a hunched posture, a tucked tail, lifting paws, and slowing down or turning for home. The more concerning stage is when shivering stops while the animal is still cold — that, with lethargy, stumbling, weakness or shallow breathing, points to hypothermia and needs a vet rather than just a warm room. Frostbite usually hits ear tips, tail tip and paws, may look pale or grey before it looks painful, and should be warmed gradually rather than rubbed or heated directly.
