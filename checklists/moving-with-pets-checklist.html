Frequently Asked Questions

What's the most commonly forgotten step when moving with a pet?

Updating the microchip registration to the new address. It's easy to focus on physically getting the pet to the new home and forget that if they escape during the move — which is when escapes are most likely — an outdated microchip address makes it much harder for anyone to reunite you. Update it as soon as you have a confirmed new address, not after you've settled in.

How do I keep my pet safe on moving day itself?

Confine them to one room that movers won't need to access, with food, water, bedding, and a familiar toy, and post a sign asking people not to open the door. Moving day is the highest-risk window for a pet to escape, since doors stay open for hours and strangers are moving in and out constantly — a contained, quiet space removes that risk almost entirely.

Should I let my pet explore the whole new home right away?

No — set up one safe room first with all their essentials, and let them explore the rest of the home gradually over the following days as they seem comfortable. Giving full access immediately can be overwhelming, and for cats especially, a slower introduction to the space reduces stress and the odds of them bolting or hiding somewhere hard to reach.

Why is my cat suddenly not using the litter box after moving?

A move is one of the most common triggers for litter box regression, since cats are highly sensitive to changes in territory and routine. Keeping the same litter box and litter brand, placing it in a quiet accessible spot right away, and not rushing full access to the home all help. For a deeper look at why this happens and how to fix it, see our guide on litter training regression.

When is it safe to let my cat outside after moving?

Most guidance recommends keeping cats strictly indoors for at least 2-3 weeks after a move, giving them time to bond with the new home as their territory before they have the option to roam. Letting them out too soon increases the risk they'll try to return to the old address or get lost in unfamiliar surroundings.

Do I need to check local pet laws before moving to a new area?

Yes — pet registration requirements, leash laws, and breed-specific restrictions all vary by city, county, and state, and rules that were fine where you lived before aren't guaranteed to apply at your destination. Research your new area's requirements before moving day, since some areas have registration deadlines that start counting as soon as you arrive.
