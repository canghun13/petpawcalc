If your dog recently mated, you're probably wondering: how long until puppies? The short answer is about **63 days** — but knowing *what's happening* each week makes a big difference in how well you can care for her.

This guide walks you through every stage of dog pregnancy, from fertilization to the whelping box.

---

## How Long Is a Dog's Pregnancy?

Dog gestation averages **63 days from the mating date**, with a normal range of 58–68 days. That's roughly 9 weeks — divided into three trimesters of about 21 days each.

Breed size plays a small role: smaller breeds tend to deliver slightly earlier (around day 62), while giant breeds may go a day or two longer (day 64–65). But individual variation matters more than size.

## Week-by-Week: What's Happening

### Weeks 1–2: Fertilization & Implantation
After mating, fertilization occurs within a few days. The fertilized eggs travel to the uterus and begin implanting around day 10–12. There are almost no outward signs during this period — your dog will look and act completely normal.

**What to do:** Keep her routine the same. No need to change her diet or limit exercise yet.

### Week 3: Early Signs Appear
By day 21, embryos are fully implanted. Some dogs begin showing subtle signs: slightly enlarged nipples, increased affection, or mild nausea. A vet can now confirm pregnancy with a blood test measuring the relaxin hormone.

**What to do:** If you want early confirmation, schedule a vet visit this week.

### Week 4: Ultrasound Week
The puppies' organs begin forming, and an ultrasound can now confirm pregnancy and estimate the number of fetuses. Your dog's belly may start to look slightly rounder, and her appetite will likely increase.

**What to do:** Book your first prenatal vet visit. Start transitioning to a high-quality puppy or pregnancy formula food if your vet recommends it.

### Week 5: Visible Belly Growth
This is when things become obvious. Your dog's abdomen rounds out noticeably as the puppies grow rapidly. Appetite increases significantly. She may sleep more and want more downtime.

**What to do:** Gradually increase her food portions — up to about 1.5x her normal amount by the end of pregnancy. Always keep fresh water available.

### Week 6: Slowing Down
The puppies' coats and claws are forming. Your dog will tire more quickly and may prefer shorter, gentler walks. Her belly will be unmistakably large by now.

**What to do:** Split her daily food into 2–3 smaller meals instead of one large one. Avoid roughhousing or strenuous activity.

### Week 7: Set Up the Whelping Box
Puppies are nearly fully developed. You may start to feel or see movement in your dog's belly. Nesting behavior — scratching, circling, rearranging bedding — often begins this week.

**What to do:** Set up the whelping box now and introduce your dog to it. A simple, clean, low-sided box lined with washable bedding works well. Schedule an X-ray this week to count the puppies — this is important so you'll know when delivery is complete.

### Week 8: Final Preparations
The puppies are fully formed and moving into birth position. Your dog may become restless, seek out quiet spots, or refuse food as her body prepares for labor.

**What to do:** Take her temperature twice daily with a rectal thermometer. A normal dog temperature is 101–102.5°F (38.3–39.2°C). When it drops below 99°F (37.2°C), labor usually begins within 12–24 hours.

### Week 9: Labor & Delivery
From day 58 onward, birth can happen at any time. Most deliveries take 3–12 hours total, with each puppy born 30–60 minutes apart. It's normal for your dog to rest between puppies.

**What to do:** Stay close, stay calm, and have your vet's emergency number ready. Healthy puppies should begin nursing within a couple of hours of birth.

## Early Signs of Pregnancy in Dogs

If you're not sure whether your dog is pregnant, watch for these signs in the first few weeks:

- **Reduced appetite or mild vomiting** (early weeks)
- **Nipple enlargement and color change** (around week 3–4)
- **Increased clinginess or affection**
- **Lethargy and more sleep than usual**
- **Clear vaginal discharge** (week 4 onward — normal, unless colored or foul-smelling)

The only reliable way to confirm pregnancy is through a vet — either a blood test (week 3+), ultrasound (week 4+), or X-ray (week 7+).

## How to Prepare: A Simple Checklist

- [ ] Confirm pregnancy with vet (week 3–4)
- [ ] Switch to puppy/pregnancy food (week 4–5)
- [ ] Set up whelping box (week 7)
- [ ] Get an X-ray to count puppies (week 7–8)
- [ ] Take daily temperature readings (week 8–9)
- [ ] Have vet's emergency number saved

## What to Feed a Pregnant Dog

During the first 4–5 weeks, your dog's calorie needs don't change much. After that, gradually increase her food — a high-quality puppy formula works well because it's calorie-dense and nutritionally complete.

By the last trimester, she may need up to twice her normal daily calories. Feed smaller, more frequent meals as her belly grows and her stomach has less room.

Avoid calcium supplements during pregnancy unless specifically recommended by your vet — too much calcium can actually cause problems during delivery.

---

## When to Call the Vet

Call your vet immediately if:
- Labor hasn't started by day 68
- More than 4 hours pass between puppies during delivery
- Your dog strains hard for over 30 minutes without producing a puppy
- You see green or black discharge before the first puppy arrives
- A puppy appears stuck
