A dog's yearly cost depends far more on size, age and what you choose to pay for than on any single "average". Survey figures give a useful frame, though, and national price averages show where the money goes. This guide uses three: Rover's 2026 cost of pet ownership report, the ASPCA's annual cost table, and a 2025–26 national study of veterinary prices run for CareCredit by Synchrony.

## The Headline Numbers

| Measure | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Medium-sized dog, per month | About $145–$505 | Rover, 2026 |
| Senior dog, per year | About $2,475–$5,200 | Rover, 2026 |
| Median lifetime cost, medium dog | $39,350 | Rover, 2026 |
| Typical later year (baseline) | $1,391 | ASPCA (2021 figures) |
| First year | $3,221 | ASPCA (2021 figures) |

Rover found that small-breed adults cost about **63% less a year** than large and giant breeds, and that yearly spending drops about **21% from the puppy year to adulthood**. In its May 2026 survey of 1,000 US pet owners, 83% said they had noticed pet costs rising over the past year — one reason the ASPCA's 2021 table is best treated as a floor rather than today's price.

## Where the Money Goes

The ASPCA's table breaks a typical year into categories (2021 figures):

| Category | Per year |
|---|---|
| Food | $300 |
| Routine medical care (exams, vaccines) | $225 |
| Preventive medication (heartworm, flea and tick) | $185 |
| Health insurance | $516 |
| Treats | $60 |
| Toys | $37 |
| Grooming supplies | $28 |
| License | $15 |
| Boarding (one day) | $25 |
| **Total** | **$1,391** |

The ASPCA counts boarding at $25 a day, so more nights away add to the total. Professional grooming ($300) and dental care ($500) appear in its first-year figures. Food is the line that varies most with size — a 90 lb dog needs roughly five times the calories of a 10 lb dog (see [How Much Should I Feed My Dog?](/blog/how-much-to-feed-a-dog/)) — and grooming varies most with coat type.

## Veterinary Care: What Each Item Costs

Synchrony's 2025–2026 study of veterinary practices across all 50 states gives these national averages for dogs:

| Service | Average cost |
|---|---|
| Routine exam | $56–$129 |
| DHPP vaccine | $42 |
| Rabies vaccine (one-year) | $35 |
| Leptospirosis vaccine | $34 |
| Bordetella (kennel cough) vaccine | $39 |
| Canine influenza vaccine | $53 |
| Chemistry panel / complete blood count | $164 / $104 |
| X-rays (two to three views) | $172 |
| Dental cleaning | $379 |

A healthy adult dog's routine year is usually an exam, whichever vaccines are due, a heartworm test and monthly preventives. Older dogs add bloodwork, and many need dental cleanings — see [Dog and Cat Dental Cleaning Cost](/blog/dog-cat-dental-cleaning-cost/). PetMD's veterinarian-written guide recommends checkups every six months once a dog is a senior, which starts around 7 for medium-sized dogs and a year or two earlier for large and giant breeds.

## Pet Insurance

According to the North American Pet Health Insurance Association, the average US premium in 2025 was:

| Plan type | Dogs, per year |
|---|---|
| Accident and illness | $836 (up 11.5%) |
| Accident only | $190 |
| Insurance with embedded wellness | $1,414 |

Premiums rise with age and vary by breed and location. Accident-and-illness plans don't cover routine care; wellness cover costs extra. Our [Pet Insurance Cost Estimator](/tools/pet-insurance-cost-estimator.html) shows how age and deductible change the price.

## Emergencies

Emergencies are what turn a predictable year into an expensive one. Approximate ranges at emergency hospitals, compiled by Preventive Vet:

| Situation | Approximate cost |
|---|---|
| Emergency exam | $100–$200 |
| Hospitalization, 1–2 days (vomiting, diarrhea, seizures) | $600–$1,700 |
| Hospitalization, 3–5 days (parvo, kidney failure) | $1,500–$3,500 |
| Bloat (GDV) | $2,000–$7,500+ |
| Intestinal obstruction with surgery | $3,000–$4,000+ |
| Grape or raisin ingestion | $2,000–$5,000+ |
| Hit by a car | $250–$8,000+ |

Synchrony's study puts the average emergency-clinic hospitalization for a dog at $1,323.

## Why the First Year Costs More

The ASPCA's first-year list adds one-time costs to the usual annual ones:

| One-time cost | ASPCA (2021) |
|---|---|
| Spay or neuter | $300 |
| Initial medical care (vaccine series and exams) | $300 |
| Training classes or materials | $200 |
| Collar, leash and harness | $60 |
| Crate | $60 |
| Carrier | $50 |
| Grooming tools | $40 |
| Microchip | $20 |

Current spay and neuter prices are higher: Synchrony's averages are $455 for spaying a small or medium dog and $487 for a neuter (see [Spay and Neuter Cost](/blog/spay-neuter-cost-and-timing/)). A dog from a registered breeder costs about $675–$4,440 to buy, according to Rover; shelter adoption fees are usually lower and often include vaccines and spay or neuter.

## Keeping Costs Down

The ASPCA's suggestions include comparing fees for preventive care between practices, discussing which vaccines your dog actually needs, brushing teeth at home with a pet toothpaste, using parasite products exactly as directed, trimming nails and brushing at home, and buying insurance while your dog is young and healthy. [How to Reduce Vet Costs for Dogs](/blog/how-to-reduce-vet-costs-for-dogs/) goes through each.

## Checking the ASPCA Figure Against Today's Prices

The ASPCA's $225 for routine medical care uses 2021 figures. Building the same year from the 2025–26 averages and ranges above gives a useful cross-check for a healthy adult dog:

| Item | Cost this year |
|---|---|
| Routine exam | $56–$129 |
| Rabies (one-year vaccine) | $35 |
| Leptospirosis (yearly) | $34 |
| DHPP ($42 every three years, spread over three) | about $14 |
| Heartworm test (PetMD range) | $20–$75 |
| Fecal exam (PetMD range) | $25–$50 |
| **Total** | **about $185–$340** |

The ASPCA's $225 falls inside that range but towards the lower end, and the range leaves out Bordetella, influenza and Lyme vaccines, which add $39–$53 each if your dog needs them. Using a three-year rabies vaccine where your state allows it lowers the total a little. The point of the check is that a budget built on older averages can come in low, so add a margin to the yearly figure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does it cost per month to own a dog?**
Rover's 2026 cost of pet ownership report puts a medium-sized dog at about $145–$505 a month. The low end is roughly food, preventives and routine vet care; the high end includes insurance, grooming, dog walking or boarding. Small breeds cost less — Rover found small-breed adults cost about 63% less a year than large and giant breeds.

**What is the most expensive year of dog ownership?**
Usually the first, because of one-time costs such as spay or neuter, the puppy vaccine series, microchipping, a crate and training. The ASPCA's cost table puts a dog's first year at $3,221 against $1,391 for a typical later year (2021 figures). The senior years can cost even more: Rover estimates $2,475–$5,200 a year for a senior dog.

**How much does pet insurance cost for a dog?**
The average US accident-and-illness policy for a dog cost $836 a year in 2025, according to the North American Pet Health Insurance Association — up 11.5% on the year before. Accident-only cover averaged $190. Whether it pays off depends on your dog and your savings: a single emergency surgery such as bloat commonly costs $2,000–$7,500 or more.

**How much should I save for a dog emergency fund?**
Enough to cover at least an emergency exam and a short hospital stay. Preventive Vet's emergency price ranges put an ER exam at $100–$200 and one to two days in hospital at $600–$1,700; emergency surgery commonly costs $1,500–$5,000. If you have insurance, the fund also covers the deductible and the bill until you're reimbursed.

**How much does a dog cost over its lifetime?**
Rover's 2026 report estimates a median lifetime cost of $39,350 for a medium-sized dog. That includes the higher-cost first year and senior years, so the figure for a small, healthy dog will be lower and for a large breed with health problems higher.

*Costs are US figures from the sources named and vary by location, clinic, breed and individual dog. The ASPCA figures date from 2021; current prices are generally higher.*
