A single box of flea and tick chews can cost well over $100, but the useful number is the yearly total — and that varies several-fold depending on the type of product. Below are actual retail prices from Chewy, a large US online pet pharmacy, checked in November 2026. Your clinic's or local store's prices may differ.

## What a Year Costs: Dogs

For a medium-sized dog (about 23–60 lb, depending on the product's weight bands):

| Product | Type | Covers | Price checked | Per month | Per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seresto (over 18 lb) | Collar, 8 months | Fleas, ticks | $57.98 per collar | about $7 | about $87 |
| Frontline Plus (23–44 lb) | Monthly topical | Fleas, ticks | $68.98 for 6 doses | about $11.50 | about $138 |
| NexGard (24.1–60 lb) | Monthly chew (prescription) | Fleas, ticks | $167.89 for 6 chews | about $28 | about $336 |
| Simparica Trio (22.1–44 lb) | Monthly chew (prescription) | Fleas, ticks, heartworm, roundworms, hookworms | $228.13 for 6 chews | about $38 | about $456 |

Every one of these is priced by weight band, so a small dog pays less and a giant breed more. Autoship or larger packs lower the price a little. Our [Flea & Tick Prevention Cost Calculator](/tools/flea-tick-prevention-cost-calculator.html) works out a yearly total for your pet.

## What a Year Costs: Cats

For an average-sized cat, Revolution Plus (5.6–11 lb) — a prescription monthly topical that covers fleas, ticks, heartworm, ear mites, roundworms and hookworms — was **$154.40 for 6 doses** at Chewy, or about **$26 a month** and **$309 a year**. Flea-only and over-the-counter cat products cost less.

The ASPCA's advice is to use parasite products exactly as directed and **never to use a dog product on a cat**. Some dog products contain permethrin, which is highly toxic to cats.

## Comparing Like With Like

The cheapest product per month isn't always the cheapest overall:

- **Heartworm.** Dogs and cats in most of the US also need heartworm prevention. A combination chew such as Simparica Trio replaces a separate heartworm preventive; a year of a popular monthly heartworm chew for a 51–100 lb dog costs about $146 at the same pharmacy (see [ProHeart 12 Cost](/blog/proheart-12-cost/)). Compare the combination price with flea-and-tick plus heartworm bought separately.
- **Ticks in your area.** Products differ in which tick species they cover and for how long; your vet will know the local risks.
- **Missed doses.** A collar or a long-acting option protects through months when a monthly dose would be forgotten.

## Why Vets Recommend Year-Round Prevention

The Companion Animal Parasite Council recommends **year-round, lifelong** flea and tick prevention. Its reasons include:

- by the time owners notice fleas, they may already have transmitted infections and begun laying eggs,
- ticks can transmit disease before they're found and removed,
- flea and tick seasons are shifting and vary by region, so start and stop dates are hard to set,
- no product prevents every tick-borne disease, so consistent use matters.

Heartworm is the other reason not to stop. Treating heartworm in a dog usually costs **$700–$2,000 or more** (see [Heartworm Treatment Cost](/blog/heartworm-treatment-cost/)), and there is no approved drug treatment for heartworm infection in cats, which makes prevention the only option for them.

## The Once-a-Year Injection for Dogs

On July 10, 2025, the FDA approved **Bravecto Quantum** (fluralaner extended-release injectable suspension), the first long-acting injectable flea and tick preventive for dogs. A single injection, given by a veterinarian:

- protects against fleas and most tick species for **12 months**, including the black-legged, American dog, brown dog, Asian longhorned and Gulf Coast ticks,
- protects against the **lone star tick for 8 months**, so dogs exposed to it are re-dosed every 8 months,
- is approved for dogs and puppies **6 months and older**, and **not for cats**.

The FDA notes that isoxazoline products, the class fluralaner belongs to, have been associated with neurologic reactions including **muscle tremors, ataxia and seizures** in some dogs and cats, so your vet will review your dog's history first. Because the dose is weight-based and the injection is given in the clinic, the price is set by each practice; ask for a quote and compare it with a year of your current product.

## If You Already Have Fleas

Prevention alone may not clear an established infestation quickly, because eggs and larvae live in the home rather than on the pet. The CAPC notes that with treated pets acting as "flea vacuums", clearing an infestation can take **four to eight weeks or longer**. In practice that means:

1. treating **every pet in the household**, not just the one with visible fleas,
2. washing pet bedding and vacuuming carpets and furniture often throughout that period,
3. keeping every pet on prevention afterwards, so the problem doesn't return.

Your vet can recommend a fast-acting product and check for flea allergy or skin infection, which flea-allergic pets often need treated as well. Our [Pet Itch Score Tracker](/tools/pet-itch-score-tracker.html) helps you record how itchy your pet is.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does flea and tick prevention cost per month?**
For a medium-sized dog at one large online pharmacy, about $11.50 a month for Frontline Plus, about $7 a month for a Seresto collar (8 months per collar), about $28 a month for NexGard chews and about $38 a month for Simparica Trio, which also prevents heartworm. Prices are by weight band, so bigger dogs pay more.

**Why do some flea and tick products cost so much more than others?**
Mostly because of what they cover and how they're given. Prescription chews such as NexGard work for a month at a time and kill ticks as well as fleas; combination chews such as Simparica Trio also prevent heartworm and treat some intestinal worms. Older over-the-counter topicals and collars cost less per month.

**Can I use dog flea and tick medication on a cat?**
No. The ASPCA warns never to use a dog product on a cat. Some dog products contain permethrin, which is highly toxic to cats. Use only a product labelled for cats, at the dose for your cat's weight.

**Do pets need flea and tick prevention all year?**
The Companion Animal Parasite Council recommends year-round, lifelong prevention. Its reasons include that by the time fleas are noticed they may already have laid eggs, ticks can transmit disease before they're found, and flea and tick seasons are shifting and vary by region.

**Is there a once-a-year flea and tick shot for dogs?**
Yes. In July 2025 the FDA approved Bravecto Quantum, a fluralaner injection given by a vet that protects dogs 6 months and older against fleas and most ticks for 12 months, and lone star ticks for 8 months. It isn't approved for cats. Like other isoxazoline products, it carries a warning about possible neurologic reactions such as tremors and seizures.
