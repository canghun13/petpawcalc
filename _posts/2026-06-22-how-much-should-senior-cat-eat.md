Feeding an older cat isn't just "less food for a less active cat." Cats are unusual: their energy needs **fall through middle age, then rise again** in later years, and many older cats digest protein and fat less efficiently. A cat that's fed less because it's "getting old" can end up losing muscle it can't afford to lose.

## When Is a Cat Senior?

According to VCA Hospitals' veterinary nutritionists, cats are **mature at about 7–10 years** and **senior from about 10**, following the Feline Veterinary Medical Association. Some cats, depending on breed or health, are considered senior earlier.

## How Needs Change With Age

| Age | What tends to happen |
|---|---|
| 7–10 years | Many cats gain weight; energy needs fall |
| From about 10–11 years | Energy needs **start to rise again** (AAHA puts the turn at about 10; VCA at about 11) |
| Older seniors | Many need **more calories and protein** to keep a normal body shape; muscle loss becomes common |

Two findings explain why older cats need careful feeding. The 2023 AAHA Senior Care Guidelines report that **protein is less digestible in about 20% of cats over 11, and fat in up to a third**, and that **sarcopenia can cause up to 33% loss of lean body mass** in cats aged 10 to 15. A review of unintentional weight loss in cats adds that older cats are more likely to be underweight than obese.

## Estimating Daily Calories

Vets start from the **resting energy requirement (RER)**:

**RER = 70 × (body weight in kg)^0.75**

The Merck Veterinary Manual's starting multiplier for a **neutered adult cat is 1.2 × RER** (1.0 for an obesity-prone cat):

| Cat's weight | RER | Starting point (1.2 × RER) |
|---|---|---|
| 3 kg (6.6 lb) | about 160 kcal | about 190 kcal |
| 4 kg (8.8 lb) | about 200 kcal | about 240 kcal |
| 4.5 kg (10 lb) | about 215 kcal | about 260 kcal |
| 5.5 kg (12 lb) | about 250 kcal | about 300 kcal |

These are starting points. For a slightly heavy 8-year-old, you may end up below them; for a thin 14-year-old, above them. The AAHA guidelines suggest an ideal body condition of up to **6 out of 9** for older cats. Our [Pet Food Calorie Calculator](/tools/pet-food-calorie-calculator.html) does the arithmetic, and VCA advises not relying on a label's feeding chart for more than a couple of weeks, because it can overestimate.

## Protein: Don't Cut It in a Healthy Senior

The AAHA guidelines say senior cats **may need up to 50% more protein** to help slow muscle loss, and that healthy senior cats may need **higher-protein, more calorie-dense, highly digestible** diets. VCA considers the same high-protein, low-carbohydrate food often fed to younger cats reasonable for healthy seniors.

Protein and phosphorus are adjusted for specific illnesses — most often kidney disease, where a kidney support diet may be prescribed. That's a decision for your vet after bloodwork, not a reason to cut protein in a healthy cat. See [Cat Kidney Disease Cost by Stage](/blog/cat-kidney-disease-cost-by-stage/).

## How Often to Feed

The AAHA guidelines note healthy senior cats do best with **smaller, more frequent meals**. VCA suggests splitting the daily amount into **two, three or four** small meals.

## Water and Wet Food

VCA calls water the most important nutrient at any age. Feeding more **canned food** increases water intake, and offering **several water bowls of different sizes on every floor**, away from food, helps cats that drink little. This matters because kidney disease is common in older cats.

## Weight Changes That Need a Vet

Weigh your cat monthly — a baby scale works, or weigh yourself holding the cat and subtract your own weight. See your vet if your senior cat:

- **loses weight or muscle**, especially along the spine and hips,
- **eats more but loses weight** — a classic pattern of [hyperthyroidism](/blog/cat-hyperthyroidism-treatment-cost/) or [diabetes](/blog/cat-diabetes-treatment-cost/),
- drinks or urinates more,
- drops food, chews on one side or eats less.

VCA recommends **twice-yearly** checkups for senior cats. More on recognising thin cats and muscle loss is in [How to Tell If Your Cat Is Underweight](/blog/how-to-tell-if-cat-is-underweight/).

## Two References, Two Starting Points

The Merck figure above is a single number. The 2021 AAHA Nutrition and Weight Management Guidelines give a range instead. For a 10 lb (4.5 kg) cat with an RER of about 218 kcal:

| Cat | Merck | 2021 AAHA guidelines |
|---|---|---|
| Neutered adult | 1.2 × RER: about 260 kcal | 1.2–1.4 × RER: about 260–305 kcal |
| Obesity-prone or inactive | 1.0 × RER: about 220 kcal | 1.0 × RER: about 220 kcal |

Merck's figure is the bottom of AAHA's range. Given how needs change with age, the low end tends to suit a 7–10-year-old that is gaining weight, while an older senior holding its weight poorly may need the upper end or more. Weighing monthly shows which way your cat is going.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many calories does a senior cat need per day?**
There's no single number. Vets start from the resting energy requirement — 70 × body weight in kg to the power 0.75 — and the Merck Veterinary Manual multiplies it by 1.2 for a neutered adult cat. For a 4.5 kg (10 lb) cat that's about 260 kcal a day as a starting point. Because older cats' needs rise again from about 10–11 years and many digest food less well, body and muscle condition should guide the final amount.

**Do senior cats need more or less food?**
Middle-aged cats often need a little less, but from around 10–11 years energy needs start to rise again, according to the 2023 AAHA Senior Care Guidelines and VCA's veterinary nutritionists. Many older cats need more calories and protein to keep a normal body shape, so an older cat losing weight shouldn't be fed less.

**Should senior cats eat less protein?**
No, not if they're healthy. The AAHA guidelines note senior cats may need up to 50% more protein to help slow muscle loss, and that protein is less digestible in about 20% of cats over 11. Protein is only adjusted for specific diseases, such as advanced kidney disease, on your vet's advice.

**How often should I feed my senior cat?**
Smaller, more frequent meals suit most healthy senior cats, according to the AAHA guidelines. VCA suggests dividing the daily amount into two, three or four small meals.

**Why is my senior cat always hungry?**
A bigger appetite in an older cat, especially with weight loss, can be a sign of hyperthyroidism, diabetes or intestinal disease. It's worth a vet visit with bloodwork rather than simply feeding more.
