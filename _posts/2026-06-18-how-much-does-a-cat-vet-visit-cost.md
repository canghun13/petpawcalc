A routine cat checkup is one of the cheaper things in pet care. The expensive cat bills come from a handful of conditions that cats hide until late. This guide covers both, using national price averages from a 2025–2026 study of veterinary practices across all 50 states run for CareCredit by Synchrony, the ranges in a veterinarian-written PetMD guide (Jennifer Coates, DVM, 2025), and emergency ranges compiled by Preventive Vet.

## What Routine Cat Care Costs

| Service | Average (Synchrony) | Range (PetMD) |
|---|---|---|
| Routine exam | $57–$130 | Office visit $40–$90 |
| FVRCP vaccine | $34 | Core vaccines $20–$60 each |
| Rabies vaccine | $31 | — |
| FeLV vaccine | $33 | — |
| FeLV/FIV test | — | $20–$40 |
| Fecal exam | — | $25–$50 |
| Urinalysis | — | $20–$60 |
| Chemistry panel | $144 | Blood work $50–$200 |
| X-rays (two to three views) | $158 | — |
| Ultrasound | $323 | — |
| Dental cleaning | $395 | — |
| Emergency exam | $143 | — |

PetMD recommends checkups every three to four weeks for kittens from 6–8 weeks until 16–20 weeks old, **once a year for adults** and **every six months for seniors** from about 9–10 years. See [How Often Do Cats Need to Go to the Vet?](/blog/how-often-vet-visits-cat/) and, for the same prices for dogs, [How Much Does a Vet Visit Cost?](/blog/how-much-does-a-vet-visit-cost/).

## A Typical Year for a Healthy Adult Cat

Using Synchrony's averages and PetMD's fecal-test range:

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Exam | $57–$130 |
| Fecal test | $25–$50 |
| FVRCP and rabies, in a year both are due | $65 |
| **Total, no vaccines due** | **about $80–$180** |
| **Total, both vaccines due** | **about $145–$245** |

Parasite prevention, if your vet recommends it, is extra — see [Flea and Tick Prevention Cost](/blog/flea-tick-prevention-cost/). For a senior cat, add bloodwork and usually a second visit each year.

## Where Cat Bills Get Big

### 1. Urinary blockage in male cats

The most important cat-specific emergency. A blocked cat can't pass urine, and the condition becomes life-threatening quickly. Treatment means hospitalization, a urinary catheter and several days of care: Preventive Vet puts urinary tract obstruction at about **$1,500–$3,000+**, and lists a 3–5 day hospital stay for a blocked cat at $1,500–$3,500.

**The sign to know:** a male cat straining in the litter box and producing little or nothing is a same-day emergency, not constipation. Our [Cat Urinary Emergency Checker](/tools/cat-urinary-emergency-checker.html) walks through the urgency question, and the [Cat Urinary Health Checklist](/checklists/cat-urinary-health-checklist.html) covers prevention.

### 2. Tooth resorption: extractions, not cleanings

Cats are prone to tooth resorption, in which the tooth breaks down and usually has to be extracted. Much of the damage sits below the gumline, so it's found on dental X-rays under anesthesia. That's why a cat dental estimate is often a range: Synchrony's averages are $395 for the cleaning, $133 for dental X-rays and $99 for each extraction, and the vet often can't say how many teeth are affected until the X-rays are taken. [Dog and Cat Dental Cleaning Cost](/blog/dog-cat-dental-cleaning-cost/) has more.

### 3. Hyperthyroidism in older cats

Up to about 10% of cats over 10 develop an overactive thyroid, according to the AAHA/AAFP feline guidelines. Weight loss despite a big appetite is the classic sign, and a T4 test — often part of a senior blood panel — picks it up. The treatment choice has a large effect on the long-run cost; [Cat Hyperthyroidism Treatment Cost](/blog/cat-hyperthyroidism-treatment-cost/) works through it.

### 4. Kidney disease monitoring

Kidney disease is common in older cats and is managed rather than cured. The cost is ongoing: repeat bloodwork and urine tests, blood pressure checks and often a kidney diet. Found early on senior bloodwork it's cheaper to manage than in a cat that arrives in crisis. See [Cat Kidney Disease Cost by Stage](/blog/cat-kidney-disease-cost-by-stage/).

### 5. Outdoor access

Cats that go outdoors face costs indoor cats mostly avoid. Preventive Vet's approximate emergency ranges include **$300–$1,500+ for a cat-bite abscess** and **$250–$8,000+ for being hit by a car**. FeLV/FIV testing and FeLV vaccination are also more often recommended for these cats.

## Other Common Cat Emergencies

From Preventive Vet's approximate ranges at emergency hospitals:

| Situation | Approximate cost |
|---|---|
| Emergency exam | $100–$200 |
| Lily ingestion | $1,000–$4,000+ |
| High-rise fall | $500–$6,000+ |
| Hospitalization, 1–2 days (vomiting, diarrhea, seizures) | $600–$1,700 |
| Intestinal obstruction with surgery | $3,000–$4,000+ |

Synchrony's study puts the average emergency-clinic hospitalization for a cat at $1,144. The North American Pet Health Insurance Association's 2025 average for an accident-and-illness policy for a cat was $435 a year.

## The Cost of a Scared Cat

A cat that is terrified of the carrier and the clinic is harder to examine, may need sedation for simple procedures, and tends to be brought in only when something is obviously wrong. The cheapest fixes come before the appointment:

- **Carrier training at home**, so the carrier stops meaning "vet". Our [Cat Carrier Training Planner](/tools/cat-carrier-training-planner.html) counts back from the appointment date.
- **A pre-visit anti-anxiety medication** for very fearful cats, which your vet can prescribe.
- **A cat-friendly clinic** with separate waiting areas and cat-focused handling.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a cat vet visit cost without insurance?**
The exam averages $57–$130, according to a 2025–2026 national study of veterinary prices run for CareCredit by Synchrony. Vaccines are extra — an FVRCP averages $34 and rabies $31 — so a checkup with both due typically comes to about $120–$195 before any tests.

**Why are cat vet bills so expensive when my cat seemed fine?**
Cats tend to hide illness, so problems are often found when treatment is more involved. The large cat bills cluster in a few conditions: urinary blockage in male cats (about $1,500–$3,000+ at an emergency hospital, according to Preventive Vet), tooth resorption needing extractions, and the thyroid and kidney disease common in older cats.

**Are vet visits cheaper for cats than for dogs?**
Individual prices are similar. Synchrony's averages for a routine exam are $57–$130 for cats and $56–$129 for dogs, and a dental cleaning averages $395 for a cat and $379 for a dog. Cats usually cost less over a year because food, preventives and weight-dosed medicines cost less.

**What tests does a new cat need?**
Vets commonly test a newly adopted cat for feline leukemia virus (FeLV) and feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) — $20–$40 in PetMD's veterinarian-written price guide — and check a stool sample for parasites ($25–$50). Kittens also need the FVRCP series and rabies, and FeLV vaccination is often recommended.

**How often should a cat see the vet?**
PetMD's guide recommends visits every three to four weeks for kittens from 6–8 weeks until 16–20 weeks old, then once a year for adult cats and every six months for seniors, from about 9–10 years.

*Prices are US averages and ranges from the sources named. Your clinic's prices may differ; ask for an itemised estimate.*
