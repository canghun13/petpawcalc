Zenrelia (ilunocitinib) is the newest daily tablet for itchy, allergic dogs. The FDA approved it in September 2024, and it works on the same family of itch signals (JAK enzymes) as Apoquel, which has been the go-to allergy tablet for over a decade.

For owners, the questions are practical: is it cheaper, does it work better, and what's the catch? The answers are "often slightly", "in one company-funded trial, yes", and "vaccinations".

## What Zenrelia Costs

Zenrelia comes in 4.8 mg, 6.4 mg, 8.5 mg and 15 mg tablets, dosed by weight and given **once a day**. Published online pharmacy prices in November 2026:

| Product | Listed price | Per tablet |
|---|---|---|
| Zenrelia 4.8 mg, 30 tablets (Chewy) | $85.07 | About $2.84 |
| Zenrelia 15 mg, 90 tablets (PetSmart) | $196.47 | About $2.18 |

So a dog on one tablet a day pays roughly **$65–$85 a month**, or about **$800–$1,040 a year**, for the drug. Larger bottles cost less per tablet. Vetster's comparison puts typical monthly Zenrelia costs at about **$40–$85 for a small dog**, **$45–$90 for a medium dog**, **$70–$130 for a large dog** and **$120–$185 for an extra-large dog**.

## Zenrelia vs Apoquel: The Price Difference

Both drugs are sold per tablet, so the monthly cost follows how many tablets your dog needs. The main structural difference is the start:

| | Zenrelia | Apoquel |
|---|---|---|
| Dosing | Once daily from day one | Twice daily for up to 14 days, then once daily |
| First month, one-tablet dog | About 30 tablets | About 44 tablets |
| Example price per tablet | About $2.18–$2.84 | About $2.94 (one hospital's published list) |
| Age | 12 months and older | 12 months and older |

At those prices, the first month for a dog needing one tablet per dose comes to roughly $65–$85 on Zenrelia versus about $129 on Apoquel; after that, about $65–$85 versus about $88. PetMD likewise reports that at label doses, Zenrelia's average monthly cost is lower than Apoquel's. For dogs whose Apoquel dose is half a tablet, the gap can narrow or reverse — see how Apoquel's dose chart works in [Apoquel vs Cytopoint Cost](/blog/apoquel-vs-cytopoint-cost/).

## What the Head-to-Head Trial Found

The main comparison is a **randomized, blinded trial of 338 dogs with atopic dermatitis** at 25 clinics in Europe (**Forster et al., 2025, *Veterinary Dermatology***). Dogs received Zenrelia once daily or Apoquel at its usual label schedule for up to 112 days:

- **Itch by day 28:** reduced by **68.2%** on Zenrelia versus **59.4%** on Apoquel — a statistically significant difference. Dogs on Apoquel showed a mild rebound in itch between days 14 and 28, around when the dose drops to once daily.
- **Itch remission by day 112:** **77%** of dogs on Zenrelia versus **53%** on Apoquel.
- **Skin lesions at day 28:** reduced by 73.2% versus 69.0% — not a significant difference, though lesion scores were lower on Zenrelia later in the study.
- **Side effects** were about as frequent in both groups; vomiting and diarrhea were the most common (13.6% on Zenrelia, 10.7% on Apoquel).

Two things to keep in mind: the study was **funded by Elanco**, Zenrelia's maker, and all authors were current or former Elanco employees; and many dogs do well on either drug. A dog that's well controlled on Apoquel doesn't necessarily need to switch.

## The Vaccine Rule — the Hidden Cost

Zenrelia's label carries a **boxed warning** that dogs may have an **inadequate immune response to vaccines**. The practical rules:

- Dogs should be **up to date on vaccines before starting**.
- Stop Zenrelia **at least 28 days, and up to 3 months, before** a vaccination.
- Don't restart it until **at least 28 days after** vaccination.

The original US label also warned of potentially fatal vaccine-induced disease with modified live vaccines, based on lab studies at three times the approved dose. In **September 2025**, the FDA approved removing that language from the US label, but the boxed warning and timing rules stayed.

What this means for cost: a dog that needs a vaccine while on Zenrelia faces **at least eight weeks off the drug**, and itchy dogs often need something else to get through that gap, so ask your vet what they'd use and budget for it. According to Vetster, Apoquel can be given before, after or at the time of vaccination. If your dog's vaccine schedule falls during allergy season, that can tip the balance. ## Who Shouldn't Take It

According to the label information summarised by PetMD:

- dogs **under 12 months old**,
- dogs with **serious infections**,
- dogs that aren't up to date on vaccinations, or that are due for vaccines soon (see above).

PetMD also notes that tumors and cancers were seen in clinical studies; ask your vet how that applies to your dog's history.

## Which Should You Choose?

Reasonable reasons to consider **Zenrelia**: once-daily dosing from the start, a slightly lower monthly cost for many dogs, and a trial suggesting better itch control than Apoquel.

Reasonable reasons to stay with **Apoquel**: your dog is already well controlled, a vaccination is coming up, or your dog's Apoquel dose is a half tablet that makes it cheaper.

Neither drug treats the cause of the allergy. Finding it — fleas, food or environmental allergies — is still worth doing. Our [Elimination Diet Trial Planner](/tools/elimination-diet-trial-planner.html) and [Pet Itch Score Tracker](/tools/pet-itch-score-tracker.html) help with that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does Zenrelia cost?**
At published online pharmacy prices, Zenrelia costs about $2.84 a tablet for the 4.8 mg strength in a 30-count bottle and about $2.18 a tablet for the 15 mg strength in a 90-count bottle. A dog on one tablet a day therefore pays roughly $65–$85 a month for the drug. The tablet strength and count depend on the dog's weight, so ask your vet which your dog needs.

**Is Zenrelia cheaper than Apoquel?**
Often a little, especially in the first month. Both are priced per tablet, but Apoquel is given twice a day for up to 14 days before dropping to once daily, while Zenrelia is once daily from the start. PetMD reports that at label doses Zenrelia's average monthly cost is lower than Apoquel's, though the difference depends on your dog's weight and pharmacy.

**Does Zenrelia work better than Apoquel?**
In a randomized, blinded trial of 338 dogs with atopic dermatitis, itch fell by 68.2% on Zenrelia versus 59.4% on Apoquel by day 28, and 77% versus 53% of dogs reached remission of itch by day 112. Skin lesion scores were similar at day 28. The study was funded by Elanco, Zenrelia's maker, and all authors were current or former Elanco employees.

**What is the Zenrelia vaccine warning?**
Zenrelia's label carries a boxed warning that dogs may not respond adequately to vaccines. It should be stopped at least 28 days, and up to 3 months, before vaccination and not restarted until at least 28 days after. In 2025 the FDA approved removing the separate warning about potentially fatal vaccine-induced disease from the US label, but the vaccine timing rules remain.

**Can puppies take Zenrelia?**
No. Zenrelia is labeled for dogs at least 12 months old, the same as Apoquel. It also shouldn't be used in dogs with serious infections. Cytopoint, an injection, doesn't have the same age restriction.

**What are the side effects of Zenrelia?**
The most common are vomiting, diarrhea, nausea, reduced appetite and tiredness. Less common effects reported include urinary tract, skin or ear infections, lower white blood cell counts, raised liver values and weight gain. In the comparison trial, side effects were about as frequent as with Apoquel.
