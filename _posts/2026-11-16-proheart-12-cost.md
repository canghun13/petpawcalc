ProHeart 12 is a once-a-year injection, given at the vet's office, that prevents heartworm disease in dogs for 12 months. The FDA approved it in July 2019, and many owners now ask whether it's worth choosing over a monthly chew.

The short answer on price: it's usually about the same. The difference is in what you're buying — a year of protection that doesn't depend on remembering a pill every month.

## What ProHeart 12 Costs

ProHeart 12 is dosed by weight and only sold through vets, so prices vary by clinic. Published clinic price lists compiled by ThePricer (updated September 2026):

| Dog's weight | ProHeart 12 injection |
|---|---|
| Up to 25 lb | About $78–$150 |
| 26–50 lb | About $105–$160 |
| 51–75 lb | About $135–$190 |
| 76–100 lb | About $154–$246 |
| Over 100 lb | About $155–$345 or more |

Because the injection is given at the clinic, it usually comes with:

- the **annual heartworm test**, typically $35–$75, and
- sometimes an **exam**, typically $50–$100, if it isn't done at your dog's yearly checkup.

Most dogs need both of those every year anyway, so many owners schedule the injection at the annual wellness visit. Zoetis also runs a rewards program that gives back a small amount per injection.

## ProHeart 12 vs Monthly Chews

Zoetis says the average price of one ProHeart 12 injection is about the same as **12 months of monthly heartworm prevention**. Published prices bear that out:

| Option | Example price for a year |
|---|---|
| Heartgard Plus, 51–100 lb, 12 chews (one large online pharmacy) | About $146 |
| Heartgard Plus, 26–50 lb (6-chew box × 2 at the same pharmacy) | About $151 |
| ProHeart 12, 26–50 lb (clinic price lists) | About $105–$160 |
| ProHeart 12, 51–75 lb (clinic price lists) | About $135–$190 |

So the decision usually isn't about price. It's about **missed months**, the **safety rules**, and **what else each product covers**.

## The Real Argument: Missed Months

Monthly prevention only works if it's given every month. In practice it often isn't:

- In a study of US clinic purchase records for dogs that were also on flea and tick products (**Lavan et al., 2018, *Parasites & Vectors***), owners bought about **7 months** of heartworm medication per dog per year on average, and only about **a third** of dogs had 11–12 months.
- A Zoetis-funded analysis of clinic records (**Mwacalimba et al., 2021, *Frontiers in Veterinary Science***) found that about half of dogs on the 6-month injection got their next injection on time, compared with about a quarter of dogs on 6-dose packs of monthly products. The study measured purchases, not doses given, and the funding should be kept in mind.

A missed month can let an infection through, and heartworm treatment usually costs **$700–$2,000 or more** — see [Heartworm Treatment Cost for Dogs](/blog/heartworm-treatment-cost/). If your household regularly forgets the monthly chew, a yearly shot can be the cheaper choice once that risk is counted.

There's also a money safety net. The Zoetis ProHeart guarantee helps pay for treatment — up to $1,000 plus the main treatment drug — if a dog becomes infected despite the injections, provided they were bought from and given by a certified practice and the required negative tests were done. Our guide to [heartworm test cost](/blog/heartworm-test-cost/) explains the testing rules.

## Who Shouldn't Get ProHeart 12

The label is specific. ProHeart 12 shouldn't be given to dogs that are:

- **under 12 months old** — dogs from 6 months can have ProHeart 6, which lasts 6 months,
- **sick, debilitated or underweight**, or with a **history of weight loss**,
- known to have **reacted to ProHeart 6 or ProHeart 12** before.

The label also advises **caution when it's given with vaccines**, because anaphylaxis and other reactions have been reported when the two are given together. Ask your vet whether to separate them, especially if your dog has had vaccine reactions.

A heartworm test is needed before starting, because ProHeart 12 doesn't kill adult heartworms. When switching from a monthly product, the injection should be given within a month of the last monthly dose so there's no gap.

## Side Effects and Safety History

The product has a history owners often ask about. ProHeart 6, the 6-month version, was approved in 2001 and **voluntarily withdrawn in 2004** after reports of serious reactions, including deaths. It returned under a risk-management program, the manufacturing process was changed to remove residual solvents that may have contributed, and the FDA reports that deaths decreased afterwards. ProHeart 12 is covered by the same program, which is why **only vets and staff who have completed specific training can give it**.

In the US field study of 297 treated dogs, compared with 296 dogs given another heartworm preventive:

| Reaction | ProHeart 12 | Comparison group |
|---|---|---|
| Vomiting | 25.3% | 26.4% |
| Lethargy | 15.5% | 11.5% |
| Diarrhea | 14.5% | 15.5% |
| Poor appetite | 13.8% | 10.5% |
| Allergic-type reactions | 2.0% | 1.4% |

Most effects were similar between the groups. Severe allergic reactions are rare but can be fatal, so watch your dog after the injection and call your vet about facial swelling, hives, vomiting, weakness or collapse.

## What It Doesn't Cover

ProHeart 12 **prevents heartworm** for 12 months and **treats hookworms present at the time of the injection**. It doesn't protect against fleas or ticks, and hookworm reinfection can happen before the year is up.

Many monthly heartworm chews also treat roundworms and hookworms every month, and some monthly combination products add flea and tick protection. If your dog needs flea and tick control anyway, compare the total — see [Flea & Tick Prevention Cost](/blog/flea-tick-prevention-cost/). ## Is It Worth It?

ProHeart 12 tends to make the most sense when:

- your dog is **an adult in good health** at a steady weight,
- monthly doses **get forgotten, spat out or skipped** in your household,
- you already go to the vet **once a year** for the test and checkup.

A monthly chew may suit better when your dog is **under a year old**, **underweight or unwell**, has had **reactions** before, or when you want **monthly intestinal worm or flea and tick control** in the same product.
