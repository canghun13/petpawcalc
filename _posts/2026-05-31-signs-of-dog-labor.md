Most dogs whelp without help. What owners need is a clear picture of what's normal at each stage — and a short list of signs that mean the vet should hear from you now, not in the morning.

## Before Labor: The Signs to Watch

- **Temperature drop.** A dog's normal rectal temperature is about **100–102.5°F**. Cornell's Riney Canine Health Center notes it can fall **below 99°F within 24 hours of birth**, but the dip lasts only about **8 hours**, so it's easy to miss with checks twice a day. Start taking her temperature in the last week of pregnancy.
- **Nesting.** Digging and rearranging bedding, seeking a quiet spot.
- **Restlessness and panting** without heat or exercise. Cornell says these first-stage signs usually begin **6–12 hours before birth**.

Our [Dog Pregnancy Calculator](/tools/dog-pregnancy-calculator.html) shows the due-date window; dogs are pregnant for about 63 days from ovulation.

## Stage 1: Getting Ready

The cervix relaxes and contractions start, but you won't see pushing yet. She'll pant, pace, dig at her bedding and may refuse food. Cornell says this stage can last **up to 24–36 hours**; the Merck Veterinary Manual gives 12–24 hours as typical.

**Call the vet if** stage 1 goes on for more than 24 hours without progress, or whelping hasn't started **24–36 hours after the temperature drop**.

## Stage 2: Delivering Puppies

Visible abdominal pushing starts, and puppies arrive in their membranes. Normal timings:

| What | Normal | Call the vet if |
|---|---|---|
| Pushing for each puppy | 0–30 minutes | Strong contractions for **more than 30 minutes** with no puppy |
| Gap between puppies | Up to **2 hours** (Merck: usually 30–60 minutes) | **More than 2 hours** between puppies |
| Total whelping | 1 to over 24 hours; ideally about 7–9 hours (Merck) | **More than 24 hours** in total |

The mother usually breaks the membrane and licks the puppy to stimulate breathing. If she doesn't, clear the membrane from the puppy's face and rub it gently with a clean towel.

Weak or no contractions for more than 2 hours with puppies still to come, or a puppy visibly stuck at the vulva, are also reasons to call.

## Stage 3: Placentas

Placentas pass with or shortly after each puppy. Cornell notes it's common to see **two puppies, then two placentas**, because puppies often come from alternate horns of the uterus. Most mothers eat the placentas, and a retained placenta is rarely a problem in dogs.

Stages 2 and 3 alternate until the last puppy is born. If you had an X-ray count late in pregnancy, compare the number born with the number expected.

## After the Birth

- **Discharge.** Dark red to black, odorless discharge (lochia) is normal and heavy for the first few days. Cornell says it usually lasts about **3 weeks**, sometimes up to 8. **Foul-smelling or increasing discharge** needs a vet.
- **Puppies' weight.** The Merck manual recommends weighing puppies twice a day for the first week; a puppy that isn't gaining needs prompt attention.

## When to Call the Vet Now

Based on Cornell and the Merck Veterinary Manual:

- **more than 30 minutes** of strong contractions without a puppy,
- **more than 2 hours** between puppies,
- **dark green or foul-smelling discharge** before the first puppy (a sign the placenta may be separating),
- **heavy fresh bleeding**,
- **no labor within 24–36 hours** of the temperature drop, or a pregnancy past **70 days** from a known breeding,
- whelping lasting **more than 24 hours**,
- the mother is **weak, collapsing or very distressed**.

Difficult birth (dystocia) affects about 5% of pregnancies overall, according to Merck, but is much more common in flat-faced and short-legged breeds such as Bulldogs, Pugs, Boston Terriers and Chihuahuas. If you have one of these breeds, talk to your vet about a birth plan well before the due date.

## What to Have Ready

- a whelping box in a quiet, warm room, introduced ahead of time so she's used to it,
- clean towels, a digital thermometer and a notebook to log times,
- your vet's number and the nearest emergency clinic's number,
- a way to get there quickly if needed.

## How Long a Whole Litter Can Take

The per-puppy rules and the total-time rule fit together like this for a litter of eight (seven gaps between puppies):

| Gap between each puppy | Time spent in gaps alone |
|---|---|
| 30 minutes (Merck: usual) | about 3½ hours |
| 60 minutes (Merck: usual) | about 7 hours |
| 2 hours every time (Cornell: upper limit of normal) | about 14 hours |

With pushing time added, a litter of eight with typical gaps is broadly in line with Merck's ideal of about 7–9 hours. If every gap ran to the two-hour limit, the whole birth would take well past that, even though no single gap broke the rule. That is why it helps to note the time each puppy arrives: a pattern of long gaps, or a total heading towards 24 hours, is worth a call even before any single rule is broken. An X-ray count tells you when the last puppy is out.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does dog labor last?**
The first stage — restlessness, nesting and panting without visible pushing — usually starts 6–12 hours before the first puppy and can last up to 24–36 hours. Once pushing starts, each puppy usually arrives within 0–30 minutes, with up to 2 hours between puppies being normal. The Merck Veterinary Manual describes total delivery as anywhere from 1 to more than 24 hours, ideally about 7–9 hours.

**What are the first signs a dog is going into labor?**
A drop in rectal temperature below 99°F within about 24 hours of birth (normal is about 100–102.5°F), followed by nesting, restlessness and panting 6–12 hours before the first puppy. The temperature dip lasts only about 8 hours, so it's easy to miss.

**When should I call the vet during whelping?**
If more than 2 hours pass between puppies, if she has strong contractions for more than 30 minutes without producing a puppy, if there's dark green or foul-smelling discharge before the first puppy, if labor hasn't started within 24–36 hours of the temperature drop, if whelping lasts more than 24 hours in total, or if she's weak, collapsing or bleeding heavily.

**What discharge is normal after a dog gives birth?**
Dark red to black, odorless discharge (lochia) is normal and heavy for the first few days. Cornell says it usually lasts about 3 weeks and can continue for up to 8. Contact your vet if it smells bad or increases.

**Is it normal for a dog to eat the placentas?**
Yes. Placentas usually pass with or shortly after each puppy, often two puppies then two placentas, and most mothers eat them. Because of that, a 'retained placenta' is rarely a concern in dogs, according to Cornell.
