Rover's figures come from bookings on its own platform, so they describe independent sitters rather than commercial kennels or pet hotels, whose prices depend on the facility and the room. Rates are usually higher in large cities and at busy times. For those, enter the quote you're given.

For many cats, staying at home with visits is less disruptive than a move to an unfamiliar place; pets that need company overnight, several walks a day or regular medication may be better suited to boarding or a house sitter.

Before You Book

  • Vaccines. Ask for the provider's list early. Many dog facilities ask for Bordetella, which the AAHA vaccination guidelines recommend for dogs that mix with other dogs; some vaccines need time to take effect.
  • How days are counted — per night or per calendar day — and the checkout time.
  • Medication. Whether they can give it, what it costs, and what happens if a dose is missed. Bring it in its original labelled packaging.
  • Holiday dates. Whether a surcharge applies and how far ahead you need to book.
  • Emergencies. Which vet they use and how they'll reach you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does dog boarding cost per night?

On Rover, the national average for boarding a dog in a sitter's home was $49.01 a night as of September 2025, and for a house sitter staying in your home $55.45 a night. Kennels and pet hotels set their own prices, which vary widely by area and by the type of room, so get a written quote.

Is a drop-in sitter cheaper than boarding?

Often, if your pet only needs one or two visits a day. Rover's national average for a drop-in visit was $21.80 as of September 2025, so two visits a day comes to about $44 — a little less than its average for overnight boarding. Pets that need company overnight, medication or several walks a day may need boarding or a house sitter instead.

Do boarding facilities require vaccinations?

Most do. Kennels and boarding facilities commonly ask for proof of rabies and core vaccines, and many dog facilities also ask for Bordetella (kennel cough), which the AAHA guidelines recommend based on a dog's exposure to other dogs. Ask each facility for its list well before you book, because some vaccines need time to take effect.

How do boarding facilities count days — by night or by calendar day?

Practice varies. Some charge per night, some per calendar day regardless of the hour, and some add a fee for late pickup. The same stay can therefore be billed as two nights or three days. Ask how days are counted and what the checkout time is before you book.
