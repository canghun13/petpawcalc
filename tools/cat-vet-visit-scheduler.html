What to Bring Up at This Visit

Based on your cat's life stage — these are the topics worth raising with your vet.

Your Cat's Recommended Visit Schedule

⚕️ This follows a general schedule for healthy cats. A cat with a chronic condition should be seen as often as your vet advises, and any cat that seems unwell should be seen whatever the date.

The Schedule This Tool Uses

The dates come from a veterinarian-written PetMD guide (2025):

  • Kittens: every 3–4 weeks from 6–8 weeks until 16–20 weeks, following the vaccine series in the 2020 AAHA/AAFP Feline Vaccination Guidelines.
  • First booster: about a year after the last kitten visit (the guidelines consider an FVRCP booster at 6 months).
  • Adults: once a year.
  • Seniors: every six months, from about 9–10 years. The AAHA/AAFP life stage guidelines count cats as senior from 10, and VCA's veterinary nutritionists also recommend twice-yearly checkups for senior cats. The calculator switches at 10.

Indoor Cats Still Need the Yearly Visit

Indoor cats rarely look ill, but they're not protected from the commonest problems. The Merck Veterinary Manual says up to 70% of cats have some periodontal disease by age 2, and weight gain is common. Indoor cats may need fewer lifestyle vaccines: FeLV is recommended for kittens because their future lifestyle isn't known, and for adults it depends on whether they go outdoors or live with cats that do.

What Senior Visits Add

Senior visits usually add bloodwork, a urinalysis and a blood pressure check. Hyperthyroidism affects up to about 10% of cats over 10, according to the AAHA/AAFP guidelines, and kidney disease and diabetes also become more common with age. Tell your vet about any change in water intake, appetite, weight, litter box habits or grooming.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I take my cat to the vet?

A veterinarian-written PetMD guide recommends every 3–4 weeks for kittens from 6–8 weeks until 16–20 weeks, about a year after the last kitten visit, then once a year for adults and every six months for seniors from about 9–10 years. A cat that seems unwell should be seen whatever the schedule.

What happens at a cat's annual wellness exam?

Weight and body condition, heart and lungs, teeth and gums, eyes, ears, skin and coat, and any changes you've noticed at home, plus whichever vaccines are due. Senior visits usually add bloodwork, a urinalysis and a blood pressure check.

At what age should cats start getting checkups every 6 months?

From about 9–10 years, according to PetMD; the AAHA/AAFP life stage guidelines count cats as senior from 10. Conditions such as hyperthyroidism, which affects up to about 10% of cats over 10, kidney disease and diabetes become more common with age.
