How the Cat Heat Cycle Works

Cats are seasonally polyestrous: they come into heat again and again during the breeding season. VCA Hospitals says that in the Northern Hemisphere cats usually cycle from January to late fall, while cats in tropical regions or those kept mainly indoors may cycle year-round.

Cats are also induced ovulators. According to VCA, most need three to four matings within 24 hours to ovulate, and once ovulation happens she goes out of heat within a day or two. If she isn't mated, she goes out of heat, has a short non-receptive break, and then comes back into heat.

How Long Each Part Lasts

Heat (estrus): about 7 days on average, with a range of 1 to 21 days (VCA). This is when she is receptive to mating.

The break between heats: if she isn't mated, about 7 days on average, with a range of 2 to 19 days (VCA).

The whole cycle: about one to six weeks, averaging about three weeks (VCA). That three-week average is what the calculator uses for the next start date, with the shortest and longest typical gaps shown as a range.

Signs Your Cat Is in Heat

  • Being more affectionate and demanding attention
  • Rolling on the floor
  • Raising her rear end when stroked along the back
  • More vocalizing
  • Urinating more often, or spraying urine on vertical surfaces

If You're Not Planning to Breed

VCA says puberty, and so the first heat, typically comes at around six months of age, and that spaying is best done before the first heat — most vets recommend about six months. Many vets now recommend spaying by five months ("fix by five"), and Swedish insurance data found pyometra, a serious uterine infection, in about 2.2% of unspayed cats by age 13.

Until she is spayed, keep her indoors and away from intact males during every heat.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often do cats go into heat?

During the breeding season, about every three weeks on average if she isn't mated; VCA Hospitals gives a whole cycle of about one to six weeks. In the Northern Hemisphere cats usually cycle from January to late fall, and indoor cats may cycle year-round.

How long does a cat stay in heat?

About 7 days on average, with a range of 1 to 21 days, according to VCA. If she isn't mated, a non-receptive break of about 7 days (2–19) follows before the next heat.

When do cats have their first heat?

Typically around six months of age, according to VCA, though it varies with daylight hours. VCA says spaying is best done before the first heat.

Why does mating end a cat's heat?

Cats are induced ovulators. VCA notes most need three to four matings within 24 hours to ovulate, and once ovulation happens she goes out of heat within a day or two.
