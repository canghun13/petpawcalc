Housetraining Timeline by Breed Size

The hourly bladder rule is the same for every puppy, but the total time to full reliability varies a lot by adult size — smaller dogs simply take longer, on average, to become fully housetrained.

Adult sizeBladder maturityBasic reliabilityFull reliability
Toy / Small (under 25 lbs)5–6 months6–9 months9–12 months
Medium (25–50 lbs)4–5 months4–6 months6–9 months

"Basic reliability" means rare accidents with a consistent schedule. "Full reliability" means no exceptions even with a less rigid schedule or being left alone for a normal workday — this takes longer for every breed than most owners expect.

Why Isn't My Puppy Housetrained Yet?

Work through these before assuming training has failed:

  • The schedule hasn't kept up with growth — a bladder-hold time that was right at 8 weeks is too long by 4 months. Recalculate as your puppy grows.
  • Routine or environment changed — a move, new pet, or schedule shift (school, new job) commonly causes temporary regression even in previously reliable puppies.
  • Supervision gaps — most "random" accidents happen when the puppy is briefly out of sight. Use baby gates or a leash indoors during active training.
  • Punishment after the fact — scolding for an accident found later doesn't teach anything except to hide from you; it can make a puppy eliminate secretly rather than stopping the behavior.
  • A medical cause — sudden regression after a period of reliability, straining, blood in urine, or drinking noticeably more water are reasons to see a vet rather than retrain.
  • Breed-size expectations mismatch — a 5-month-old toy breed and a 5-month-old large breed are not at the same stage; check the timeline table above before assuming something is wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long can a puppy hold its pee?

The standard rule vets and trainers use is age in months plus one hour, capped around 8 hours for any puppy. So a 2-month-old puppy can hold it for roughly 3 hours during the day, a 4-month-old about 5 hours. Overnight, most puppies can hold it 1–2 hours longer than their daytime maximum since their metabolism slows during sleep.

How often should I take my puppy out to pee by age?

In addition to the age-based hourly limit, take your puppy out after four fixed triggers regardless of the clock: immediately after waking up, within 10–15 minutes of eating, after every nap, and after every play session. For an 8–10 week old puppy this usually adds up to 8–10 potty trips a day; by 5–6 months it's typically down to 4–6.

Why is my 6-month-old puppy still having accidents?

This is common and doesn't always mean training failed. The most frequent causes are: the schedule hasn't kept up with a growing bladder capacity (revisit the hourly rule), a change in routine or environment, incomplete supervision (accidents happen out of sight), or a urinary tract infection — which is worth ruling out with a vet if accidents suddenly increase after a period of reliability. Toy and small breeds also commonly take 6–12 months to be fully reliable, versus 4–6 months for medium and large breeds, so a 6-month-old toy breed may simply not be there yet.

Do small dogs take longer to potty train than large dogs?

Generally yes. Toy and small breeds have smaller bladders relative to their metabolism, are harder to supervise closely (an accident is easy to miss in a small space), and are often not taken outside as consistently since owners may use pads indoors. Toy/small breeds commonly take 6–12 months to be fully reliable, while medium, large, and giant breeds are often reliable by 4–6 months.

Should I crate train or pad train my puppy?

Crate training tends to housetrain faster because it leverages a puppy's instinct not to soil their sleeping area, which builds bladder control. Pad training is useful for apartments, very young puppies who can't yet make it outside, or households that are away long hours, but it can extend the timeline to outdoor reliability since it teaches the puppy that indoor elimination is sometimes acceptable. Many owners use both: pads for overnight or long absences early on, transitioning to outdoor-only as bladder control improves.

How long does it take to fully potty train a puppy?

Most puppies reach basic reliability by 4–6 months old with a consistent schedule, though occasional setbacks through the first year are normal. Toy and small breeds often take 6–12 months. Full, no-exception reliability (including when left alone for normal work hours) is usually closer to 8–12 months for any size.
