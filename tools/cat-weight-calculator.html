⚖️ Cat Weight Calculator

Compare your cat's weight with typical adult weights for its breed, or estimate an overweight cat's ideal weight from its body condition score.

⚕️ This tool gives general estimates. Individual cats vary in frame and muscle, and an overweight cat's diet plan should be set by your veterinarian.

Breed Weights Are a Reference, Not a Target

The breed ranges in this calculator come from the Association for Pet Obesity Prevention's breed weight reference and, for domestic shorthairs, PetMD. They describe what adults of each breed usually weigh. A cat can sit inside its breed range and still be overweight, or outside it and be perfectly lean, depending on its frame — which is why the result always points you to a body condition check.

Weight problems are common: in APOP's 2022 survey, about 61% of US cats were classified as overweight or obese.

Scoring Body Condition at Home

Vets use a 9-point body condition score with 5 as ideal, following the WSAVA chart. Three checks get you most of the way:

  • Ribs: run flat hands along the sides. At a 5 you feel the ribs under a slight fat covering.
  • Waist: from above, there should be a waist behind the ribs.
  • Belly: from the side, a 5 has only a minimal belly fat pad. A loose flap of skin between the hind legs — the primordial pouch — is normal in many cats and isn't the same as fat.

The 2014 AAHA Weight Management Guidelines say each point above 5 represents roughly 10% excess weight, and recommend that cats lose no more than about 0.5–2% of body weight a week. A 14 lb cat scoring 7, for example, has an estimated ideal weight of about 11.7 lbs and should lose roughly 1–4.5 oz a week. How to Tell If Your Cat Is Overweight describes each score in detail.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should a cat weigh?

Most domestic shorthair cats weigh about 8–12 lbs, according to PetMD, but breeds differ widely — from about 5–7 lbs for a female Devon Rex to 20 lbs or more for a male Maine Coon, in the Association for Pet Obesity Prevention's breed reference. Body condition is a better guide to whether an individual cat is at a healthy weight.

How does the ideal weight estimate work?

On the 9-point body condition scale, 5 is ideal, and the 2014 AAHA Weight Management Guidelines say each point above 5 represents roughly 10% excess weight. The calculator divides your cat's weight accordingly and applies AAHA's safe rate of loss for cats, 0.5–2% of body weight a week.

Why does the calculator limit how fast a cat should lose weight?

Cats that stop eating or lose weight too quickly can develop hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver disease), and overweight cats are at higher risk, according to Cornell's Feline Health Center. That's why AAHA's suggested rate for cats is slower than for dogs, and why a vet should set the calorie target.

How big will my kitten get?

There's no reliable formula for predicting a kitten's adult weight from its current weight. The breed and the parents' size are the best guides. Most cats are close to adult size by about a year, but some large breeds take much longer — Ragdolls up to four years and Norwegian Forest Cats up to five, according to APOP.

Is there a BMI for cats?

There is a research formula — the Feline Body Mass Index, based on rib cage circumference and lower leg length (Butterwick, 2000) — but vets generally use the 9-point body condition score instead, which you can check at home with your hands.
