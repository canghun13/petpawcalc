⚖️ Dog Weight Calculator

Estimate your puppy's adult weight, check how much it should be gaining each week, or estimate an overweight dog's ideal weight.

⚕️ This tool is for informational purposes only. Always consult your veterinarian for advice on your dog's growth and ideal weight.

How the Puppy Estimate Works

Puppies don't grow in a straight line — they grow fastest early and level off after about six months — so dividing weight by age and multiplying up overestimates adult size. This calculator uses the rules of thumb from a veterinarian-written PetMD guide (Sandra C. Mitchell, DVM, 2025) instead: for toy and small breeds, weight at 6 weeks × 4 or at 8 weeks × 3; for medium and large breeds, weight at 4 months × 2.

Those rules are tied to particular ages, so if your puppy is outside them the calculator tells you when to weigh again. Giant breeds have no equivalent rule. In every case, the breed and the size of the parents are good guides; paw size isn't a reliable one.

Checking Weekly Gain

The Merck Veterinary Manual says puppies gain about 2–4 g a day for each kilogram of expected adult weight during their first five months, and recommends weighing a growing puppy weekly and adjusting food so that it gains a small amount each week. A puppy heading for 50 lbs, for example, typically gains about 0.7–1.4 lbs a week in that period. A puppy that stops gaining or loses weight should see a vet.

Faster isn't better, especially for large breeds. Merck notes that overfeeding speeds growth and harms skeletal development, and that large-breed puppies fed 50–70% of what their littermates ate freely still reached normal adult size — just with less body fat.

Estimating an Adult Dog's Ideal Weight

A size category can't tell you whether a particular dog is overweight — a 55 lb dog may be lean or heavy depending on its frame. Body condition can. On the 9-point scale, 5 is ideal, and the 2014 AAHA Weight Management Guidelines say each point above 5 represents roughly 10% excess weight. A dog weighing 60 lbs at a score of 7, for instance, has an estimated ideal weight of about 50 lbs.

To score your dog, feel the ribs with flat hands, look for a waist from above and a tuck from the side. How to Tell If Your Dog Is Overweight describes each score. AAHA advises basing calories on the ideal weight and aiming for weight loss of about 1–2% of body weight a week, with your vet setting the plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the calculator estimate my puppy's adult weight?

It uses the rules in a veterinarian-written PetMD guide: for toy and small breeds, weight at 6 weeks × 4 or at 8 weeks × 3; for medium and large breeds, weight at 4 months × 2. Outside those ages it tells you when to weigh again rather than guessing.

Why won't it estimate a giant-breed puppy's adult weight?

There's no published rule of thumb for giant breeds, which keep growing until 18–24 months. The breed, the parents' weights and your vet's growth charts are better guides. The weekly-gain check still works for giant puppies if you know roughly how big they'll be.

How is the weekly gain range worked out?

The Merck Veterinary Manual says puppies gain about 2–4 g a day for each kilogram of expected adult weight during the first five months. The calculator multiplies that by your puppy's expected adult weight and by seven days.

How does the ideal weight estimate for an adult dog work?

On the 9-point body condition scale, 5 is ideal, and the 2014 AAHA Weight Management Guidelines say each point above 5 represents roughly 10% excess weight. The calculator divides your dog's current weight accordingly. For a dog scoring below 5, it doesn't estimate a target — an underweight dog needs a vet check first.

When do puppies stop growing?

Typically 8–12 months for toy breeds, 9–12 months for small, 12–15 months for medium, 15–18 months for large and 18–24 months for giant breeds, according to PetMD. Growth ends when the growth plates close, which a vet can confirm on an X-ray.
