Waiting Periods by Coverage Type

Industry ranges vary by insurer, but most policies follow this general pattern:

Coverage type Typical waiting period Why
Accidents 0-15 days Sudden and unpredictable, so there's less incentive to game the timing
Illness 14-30 days Many illnesses develop gradually, so insurers wait to rule out conditions that were already brewing before enrollment
Orthopedic (ACL/cruciate tears, hip dysplasia, etc.) 6-12 months (some insurers use 30 days or fold it into illness) These conditions can take a long time to become noticeable, and are among the most expensive claims
Wellness / preventive add-ons None — usually immediate Scheduled, non-urgent care (vaccines, annual exams) that can't be "timed" around an emergency

The Trap: Symptoms During the Waiting Period

This is the part most new pet insurance owners miss. If your pet develops symptoms of a condition — or is diagnosed with one — while a waiting period is still active, the insurer can classify it as a pre-existing condition. Once something is flagged pre-existing, it's typically excluded from that policy permanently, not just until the waiting period technically ends.

This applies even if you didn't notice or report anything at the time — if the condition is later shown (through vet records) to have started during the waiting window, a claim filed after the waiting period ends can still be denied. If you notice anything unusual during a waiting period, it's worth a vet visit and a clear paper trail, rather than waiting to see if it resolves on its own.

Switching Insurers Resets the Clock

Waiting periods belong to the policy, not the pet. If you cancel one insurer and enroll with another — even to get a better rate — all the waiting periods restart from the new policy's effective date. Any condition that developed under the old policy but wasn't yet diagnosed can also become a fresh pre-existing exclusion under the new one. Switching insurers is rarely as simple as a clean handoff.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a pet insurance waiting period?

A waiting period is the gap between your policy's effective date and the date you can actually file a claim for a given type of care. You pay premiums during this time, but a condition that develops or is diagnosed before the waiting period ends typically won't be covered.

How long are pet insurance waiting periods?

Industry-wide, accident coverage typically starts anywhere from the same day to about 15 days after enrollment, illness coverage usually starts after 14-30 days, and orthopedic conditions (like cruciate ligament tears or hip dysplasia) often have the longest wait — commonly 6-12 months, though some insurers fold this into the regular illness waiting period instead.

What happens if my pet shows symptoms during the waiting period?

This is the biggest waiting-period trap. If a condition first appears or is diagnosed while the waiting period is still active, insurers can classify it as a pre-existing condition — which is typically excluded from coverage permanently, even after the waiting period technically ends. This is true even if you didn't notice the symptoms until later.

Do waiting periods reset if I switch pet insurance companies?

Yes. Waiting periods are tied to each individual policy, so switching to a new insurer means starting the waiting period clock over from the new policy's effective date, even if your previous policy's waiting periods had already passed.

Are wellness and preventive care subject to a waiting period?

Usually not. Wellness add-ons that reimburse for routine care like vaccinations, annual exams, and spay/neuter typically start on the policy's effective date with no waiting period, since these are scheduled, non-urgent expenses rather than something an owner might rush to insure right before use.
