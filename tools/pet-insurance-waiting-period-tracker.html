What the Model Law Says

In 2022 the National Association of Insurance Commissioners adopted a Pet Insurance Model Act that states can turn into law. Its waiting-period rules:

  • Accidents: waiting periods are prohibited.
  • Illnesses and orthopedic conditions not caused by an accident: no more than 30 days.
  • Waiver: an insurer that uses waiting periods must let them be waived after a medical examination.

New Hampshire's pet insurance law, for example, includes the same three rules. If your state hasn't adopted the model act, longer waits — such as several months for orthopedic conditions — may still appear in policies, so read yours.

The Pre-Existing Condition Trap

The model act defines a pre-existing condition as one where, before the policy took effect or during any waiting period, a vet gave medical advice, the pet was treated, or records show signs or symptoms. So something that first appears during a waiting period can be excluded even after the wait ends. If you notice anything during the waiting period, ask your insurer how it will be handled before assuming it's covered.

The act also says a condition that was covered can't be reclassified as pre-existing when the policy renews.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a pet insurance waiting period?

The time between your policy's effective date and the date a given kind of condition is covered. Under the NAIC's Pet Insurance Model Act, a condition that a vet advised on or treated, or that showed signs, during a waiting period counts as pre-existing.

How long can pet insurance waiting periods be?

It depends on your state and policy. The NAIC Pet Insurance Model Act, adopted in 2022, prohibits waiting periods for accidents and caps them at 30 days for illnesses and orthopedic conditions not caused by an accident. States that adopt it, such as New Hampshire, write those limits into law; elsewhere, check your policy.

Can a waiting period be waived?

Under the NAIC model act, insurers that use waiting periods must allow them to be waived after a medical examination. Ask your insurer what the exam must include.

What happens if my pet gets sick during the waiting period?

It can be treated as a pre-existing condition. The NAIC model act's definition includes any condition for which a vet gave advice, the pet was treated, or records show signs or symptoms before the policy took effect or during a waiting period.

Does switching insurers restart waiting periods?

Waiting periods run from each policy's effective date, so a new policy starts its own. A condition that began before the new policy, or during its waiting period, can count as pre-existing under the new insurer's terms — check before you cancel the old policy.
