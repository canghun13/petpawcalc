The Microchip-Before-Rabies Rule

For Great Britain, APHIS is explicit: the rabies vaccine must be given on the same day as or after the microchip, and a vaccine given before the chip is invalid. The EU requires the vet to scan the chip before the rabies shot, and Japan requires the chip on or before the first rabies vaccination. If the order was wrong, ask your accredited vet whether revaccination is needed — and expect the waiting period to start again.

Why Japan Needs More Than Six Months

Japan requires two rabies vaccinations more than 30 days apart, a blood sample showing at least 0.5 IU/ml of rabies antibody (tested at an approved lab such as Kansas State University), and at least 180 days between the sample and arrival. The sample can be taken on the day of the second vaccination at the earliest. If fewer than 180 days have passed, the pet is held at a quarantine facility for the remaining days.

The Health Certificate Window

For non-commercial travel to the EU or Great Britain, a USDA-accredited vet issues the certificate and APHIS must endorse it within 10 days of arrival; it's valid for 30 days after the vet signs it. Commercial certificates — for example, six or more pets, or pets travelling more than five days apart from their owner — have a 48-hour window.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the microchip have to come before the rabies vaccine?

Because the destination needs to know the vaccine was given to that animal. For Great Britain, APHIS says the chip must be implanted or read on or before the primary rabies vaccination, and a vaccine given before the chip is invalid. Japan likewise requires the chip on or before the first rabies vaccination.

How long after the rabies vaccine can my pet travel to the EU or UK?

At least 21 days after the primary vaccination (the day of vaccination is day 0), or longer if the vaccine manufacturer's immunity period requires it — APHIS notes some recommend 30 days.

Why does Japan take so long?

Japan requires an ISO microchip, at least two rabies vaccinations more than 30 days apart, a rabies antibody result of at least 0.5 IU/ml, and at least 180 days between the blood sample and arrival, plus advance notification at least 40 days before arrival. Without the full 180 days, the remaining time is spent in quarantine.

When does the health certificate need to be done?

For non-commercial travel to the EU or Great Britain, a USDA-accredited vet issues the certificate and APHIS must endorse it within 10 days before arrival; it's valid for 30 days after the vet signs it. Commercial certificates have a 48-hour window.

Does my dog need a tapeworm treatment?

For Great Britain, yes: APHIS says dogs need a treatment effective against Echinococcus multilocularis, given by a USDA-accredited vet 24–120 hours (1–5 days) before entry. Within the EU, it applies only to dogs entering Finland, Malta, Ireland or Norway.
