Retained Baby Teeth: What to Check For

A retained (persistent) baby tooth is one still in place after the adult tooth has come in beside it. VCA Hospitals says it's most common in small breeds and flat-faced breeds such as Bulldogs, Pugs, Boston Terriers and Boxers, and that the upper canines are the teeth most often affected. Left in place, it can trap food and lead to tartar, gum disease and early tooth loss, and a retained lower canine can push the adult tooth toward the roof of the mouth.

VCA recommends checking your puppy's mouth weekly from the start of permanent teething until about 7–8 months, and booking a vet visit as soon as you notice a retained tooth. Early extraction usually lets the adult teeth move into the right position; delays can mean orthodontic work or a referral to a veterinary dentist.

Should you wait for the spay or neuter? VCA advises against waiting for a spay or neuter to remove a retained tooth, because extraction is needed promptly. If the surgery is already booked soon, ask your vet whether both can be done together, but don't delay the tooth to make that happen.

Choosing Chew Toys

The American Animal Hospital Association warns that hard chews such as bones, sticks, rocks, yak milk chews, antlers and rawhides can damage or fracture a dog's teeth. Its quick checks:

  • Thumbnail test: press your thumbnail into the surface; if it won't leave an indent, the toy is too hard.
  • Bend test: a safe toy should bend slightly.
  • Hammer test: if you couldn't drive a nail into it, your dog shouldn't chew it.

Supervise chewing, and ask your vet if you're unsure about a particular product for your puppy's size.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do puppies start teething?

Baby teeth start coming in at about 2–4 weeks, according to the American Kennel Club (VCA says around 3 weeks), and all 28 should be in by about 5–6 weeks.

When do puppies lose their baby teeth?

Baby teeth start to shed and adult teeth emerge at about 12–16 weeks. Most puppies have all 42 adult teeth by about 6–7 months.

What are retained baby teeth and why do they matter?

A retained baby tooth stays in place after the adult tooth has come in beside it. VCA says it's most common in small and flat-faced breeds and can trap food, leading to tartar, gum disease and early tooth loss, or push an adult tooth out of position.

Can retained baby teeth wait until the spay or neuter?

VCA advises against waiting, because extraction is needed promptly and early removal usually lets the adult teeth move into the right position. If the surgery is already booked soon, ask your vet whether both can be done together.

What's safe for a teething puppy to chew?

AAHA warns that bones, sticks, rocks, yak milk chews, antlers and rawhides can fracture teeth. A safe toy should bend slightly and take an indent when you press your thumbnail into it. Supervise chewing.
