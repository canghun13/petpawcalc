What to Bring Up at This Visit

Based on your dog's life stage — these are the topics worth raising with your vet.

Your Dog's Recommended Visit Schedule

⚕️ This follows a general schedule for healthy dogs. A dog with a chronic condition should be seen as often as your vet advises, and any dog that seems unwell should be seen whatever the date.

The Schedule This Tool Uses

The dates come from a veterinarian-written PetMD guide (Jennifer Coates, DVM, 2025):

  • Puppies: every 3–4 weeks from 6–8 weeks until 16–20 weeks old, following the vaccine series. The 2022 AAHA Canine Vaccination Guidelines call for at least three core doses between 6 and 16 weeks, 2–4 weeks apart.
  • First booster: about a year after the last puppy visit.
  • Adults: once a year.
  • Seniors: every six months.

When "senior" starts depends on size. PetMD puts it at about 7 for medium dogs, a year or two earlier for large and giant breeds, and about 9–10 for small dogs. The calculator uses 9 for small, 7 for medium and 6 for large and giant dogs.

What Each Visit Covers

A yearly exam includes weight and body condition, heart and lungs, eyes, ears, skin, joints and teeth; whichever vaccines are due; and usually a heartworm test, a review of parasite prevention and often a fecal test. Periodontal disease starts early — the Merck Veterinary Manual says up to 80% of dogs have some by age 2 — so the teeth are checked every time.

Senior visits usually add bloodwork, a urinalysis and often a blood pressure check, because conditions such as kidney disease, hypothyroidism, diabetes and Cushing's disease become more common with age and are easier to manage when found early.

How to Get the Most Out of Each Visit

Bring a short list of changes since the last visit: appetite, water intake, energy, sleep, bathroom habits and behavior. It also helps to know which vaccines are due and when heartworm and flea and tick prevention were last given.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I take my dog to the vet?

A veterinarian-written PetMD guide recommends every 3–4 weeks for puppies from 6–8 weeks until 16–20 weeks, about a year after the last puppy visit, then once a year for adults and every six months for seniors. A dog that seems unwell should be seen whatever the schedule.

What happens at a dog's annual wellness exam?

A nose-to-tail exam including weight and body condition, heart and lungs, eyes, ears, skin, joints and teeth; whichever vaccines are due; and usually a heartworm test and a review of parasite prevention. A fecal test is often included, and older dogs typically have bloodwork.

At what age should dogs start getting checkups every 6 months?

When they become seniors. PetMD puts that at about 7 for medium dogs, a year or two earlier for large and giant breeds, and about 9–10 for small dogs.
