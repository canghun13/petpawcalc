⚖️ Pet Weight Calculator

Breed charts can't tell you whether your own dog or cat is carrying too much. Body condition can. Check by hand, pick the score that matches, and get an estimated ideal weight from AAHA's guidance.

⚕️ The ideal-weight figure is an estimate from AAHA's rule of thumb, not a target your vet has set. Unexplained weight loss, or a pet scoring below 4, needs a vet check before any change in food.

How to Score Body Condition by Hand

Veterinary teams use a 9-point body condition score, with 5 as ideal (the WSAVA's charts for dogs and cats are based on Purina's). It needs your hands as well as your eyes, especially on a long or thick coat:

  • Ribs: at an ideal score you can feel them easily under a slight layer of fat, without pressing hard.
  • Waist from above: a visible narrowing behind the ribs.
  • From the side: in dogs, the belly tucks up toward the hind legs; in cats, look for a minimal belly fat pad.

Ribs that are hard to feel, no waist, or fat over the lower back and tail base point to a score above 5. Ribs, spine and hip bones that stand out point to a score below 4.

Where the Ideal-Weight Estimate Comes From

The American Animal Hospital Association's weight management guidelines treat each body condition point above 5 as roughly 10% extra body weight. A dog weighing 66 lbs at a score of 7 is about 20% over, so its estimated ideal is about 55 lbs. AAHA suggests calories are based on the ideal weight, not the current one.

For weight loss, AAHA suggests about 1–2% of body weight a week for dogs and 0.5–2% for cats, with rechecks about every two weeks. Cats must never be crash-dieted: a cat that stops eating can develop hepatic lipidosis (fatty liver disease), according to Cornell's Feline Health Center.

How Common Is It?

The Association for Pet Obesity Prevention's survey estimated that 59% of dogs and 61% of cats in the US are overweight or obese. AAHA links excess weight to shorter lifespans, orthopedic disease, diabetes and other problems.

Weight Loss Is a Reason to Call the Vet

Unplanned weight loss is worth a vet visit even when the pet seems well. In cats it can come early: a dvm360 review notes weight loss can begin about three years before a kidney disease diagnosis. Our cat and dog underweight guides cover what to look for.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy weight for my pet?

It depends on the individual, which is why vets use body condition rather than a single number. On the 9-point scale, 5 is ideal: ribs easily felt under a slight layer of fat, a visible waist from above, and in dogs a tuck from the side. Once your pet is at a 5, that weight is a sensible target.

How do I check my pet's body condition score at home?

Feel the ribs with light pressure — at an ideal score they're easy to feel under a slight layer of fat. Look from above for a waist behind the ribs, and from the side for a belly tuck in dogs or a minimal belly fat pad in cats. Then match what you find to the 1–9 descriptions.

How is the ideal weight estimated?

AAHA's weight management guidelines treat each body condition point above 5 as roughly 10% extra body weight, so the calculator divides the current weight by 1 plus 10% for each point over 5. Your vet can refine the target after an exam.
