Why Per-Incident Often Wins for a Single Chronic Condition

With an annual deductible, you pay the full deductible again every time your policy renews — even if it's the exact same ongoing condition, like diabetes or a thyroid disorder, being treated year after year. With a true per-incident (lifetime per-condition) deductible, you pay that deductible once for the diagnosed condition, and every claim related to it afterward — for the life of the policy — only costs you the co-pay percentage, with no deductible standing in the way again.

The catch: this only helps if your pet's issues stay limited to that one condition. A completely new, unrelated diagnosis triggers a brand-new deductible under a per-incident structure — so an unlucky pet with several different problems over the years can end up paying more deductibles overall than they would have under a single annual deductible that covers everything each policy year regardless of cause.

Which Structure Fits Which Situation

Situation Likely cheaper structure
One ongoing chronic condition (e.g., diabetes, hypothyroidism) Per-incident / per-condition
Generally healthy pet, occasional unrelated accidents/illnesses Annual
Multiple different chronic conditions over time Annual (each new condition resets a per-incident deductible)
Unsure / can't predict your pet's future health Annual — the more predictable, widely available default

Most insurers today only offer annual deductibles, so this comparison mainly matters if you're specifically evaluating an insurer (like Trupanion) that offers a true per-condition lifetime deductible as an alternative.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between an annual deductible and a per-incident deductible?

An annual deductible is paid once per policy year and applies to all claims that year, no matter how many different conditions come up. A per-incident (often called per-condition or lifetime per-condition) deductible is paid once for a specific diagnosed condition, and typically never again for that same condition for the life of the policy — but a completely different, unrelated condition would trigger its own separate deductible.

Which deductible type is better for a chronic condition?

For a single ongoing chronic condition, a per-incident (lifetime per-condition) deductible is usually cheaper over time, since you pay that deductible once and then only pay the co-pay percentage on treatment for that condition every year after. With an annual deductible, you pay the full deductible again every time the policy renews, even for the same ongoing condition — which adds up significantly over several years.

Are per-incident deductibles common in pet insurance?

They're less common than they used to be — most insurers have standardized on annual deductibles, and only a handful (Trupanion is the best-known example) still offer a true per-condition lifetime deductible structure. If a per-incident deductible matters to you, confirm directly with the insurer how their specific plan defines and applies it, since terminology varies.

Is an annual deductible ever the better choice?

Yes — if your pet develops several unrelated conditions over time rather than one ongoing chronic issue, an annual deductible can be simpler and cheaper, since a single deductible payment covers everything that policy year regardless of how many different problems come up. A per-incident structure means each new, unrelated condition triggers its own fresh deductible, which can add up quickly with an unlucky year.

Does my annual limit reset every policy year?

Yes, typically. Most pet insurance annual limits (sometimes called payout caps) reset at each policy renewal, similar to how a deductible resets, though limit structures vary by insurer — some plans offer no annual limit at all. Once you've reached your annual limit for the year, further claims that year usually aren't reimbursed, so it's worth tracking how much of your limit remains as the year goes on.
