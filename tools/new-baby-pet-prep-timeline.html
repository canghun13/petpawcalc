👶 New Baby Pet Prep Timeline

Enter your due date to get a dated prep plan for your dog or cat, built from PetMD's trainer-written guide, the AKC and the ASPCA — training, baby sounds, nursery rules, and a calm first meeting.

🐾 PetPawCalc

⚕️ Never leave a baby alone and unsupervised with a pet (AKC). For extreme or worrying reactions, PetMD recommends a certified behavior professional, and a vet check first, since physical problems can affect how a pet copes with stress.

Preparing a Dog

PetMD's guide (Erika Lessa, a certified behavior consultant) says to start weeks or months before the baby arrives:

  • Vet check first — physical problems can affect how a dog copes with stress.
  • Training: stay, come, go to your mat, and loose-leash walking. Mat work helps during feeds and diaper changes; verbal cues help when your hands are full. The AKC suggests a basic obedience class.
  • A safe space — a gated area with a bed, toys and chews that the baby will never use.
  • Nursery rules: decide now whether the dog may go in. If so, teach a settle on a mat there; if not, use a gate and reward the dog for waiting outside.
  • Baby sounds: recordings of crying at low volume, gradually louder, each paired with a favourite treat.
  • Rehearse: early-morning feeds, diaper changes, the swing, and carrying a realistic doll. Practise walking with an empty stroller.
  • Attention: gradually give the dog less attention at baby-focused times such as mealtimes and bedtime, so the change doesn't arrive with the baby.

Preparing a Cat

The ASPCA's timeline for cats:

  • During pregnancy: play recordings of baby noises, and rub baby lotion on your hands before playing so the scent comes with something good.
  • Several months before: if the litter box is in the nursery, move it a few inches a day — moving it faster may send the cat back to the old spot.
  • Several weeks before: set up nursery furniture and let the cat explore it before deciding which surfaces are off limits.
  • At least a month before: make off-limit surfaces unappealing, for example with double-sided tape on cardboard.
  • One to two months before: hand feeding, grooming, play and other cat care to whoever will do it after the birth.
  • After the birth: keep the cat out of the crib and sleep areas; close the nursery door at nap time or use a screen door or crib tent.

Toxoplasmosis: the ASPCA advises keeping cats indoors, scooping the litter box at least twice a day, wearing gloves for gardening and raw meat, and not feeding raw or undercooked meat. Ask your doctor about litter box duty during pregnancy.

Homecoming

  • Before the baby comes home, have someone bring a blanket or item the baby has used for your pet to sniff (AKC; PetMD; ASPCA).
  • Dogs: exercise the dog first, have someone else hold the baby while you greet the dog, toss treats on the floor to discourage jumping, and allow a sniff only when the dog is calm (PetMD).
  • Cats: greet your cat calmly in a quiet room before letting others in, and leave a used receiving blanket where it can sniff (ASPCA).
  • Keep giving your pet attention and exercise, and include it — the AKC suggests tossing treats while you change or hold the baby.

Sources: PetMD, How To Prepare Your Dog for a New Baby (Lessa, 2026); American Kennel Club, Expecting a New Human?; ASPCA, Cats and Babies. Full list on our sources page.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I start preparing my pet for a new baby?

As early as you can. PetMD's trainer-written guide says to start weeks or months before the baby arrives. For cats, the ASPCA suggests moving a litter box out of the nursery several months ahead and handing over care tasks one to two months before the birth.

How do I get my pet used to baby sounds?

Play recordings of a baby crying at low volume and gradually turn them up. PetMD suggests pairing each sound with a favourite treat and stopping the treats when the sound stops. The ASPCA suggests the same for cats during pregnancy.

How should my dog first meet the baby?

PetMD suggests exercising your dog first, having someone else hold the baby while you greet your dog, and tossing treats on the floor to discourage jumping. Allow a sniff only when your dog is calm. The AKC suggests bringing home a blanket the baby used at the hospital first.

Can my cat sleep near the baby?

No. The ASPCA advises keeping cats out of the crib and other sleep areas, and closing the nursery door during naps or using a screen door or crib tent. A newborn can't move its head, so a cat cuddling near its face could block its breathing.

Should I ever leave my pet alone with the baby?

No. The AKC says never to leave a baby alone and unsupervised with the dog, and PetMD advises always supervising dogs and babies, using gates and pens — keeping them separate, but not isolating the dog.
