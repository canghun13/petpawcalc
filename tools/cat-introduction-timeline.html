⚕️ There's no fixed schedule for cat introductions — the FelineVMA says they can take a few days to a few months. If introductions stall or cats fight, talk to your veterinarian.

Before the New Cat Arrives

  • Health first. All cats should have seen a vet recently, and the new cat should be tested for common contagious diseases (FelineVMA).
  • Resources: one litter box, food bowl, water bowl, high-sided bed and scratching post per cat, plus one extra of each, spread around the home and out of each other's sight.
  • A transition room that can be latched shut, with everything the new cat needs.
  • Pheromone diffusers: the FelineVMA suggests plugging one in the main living area and one in the transition room 24–48 hours before arrival.
  • Know what each cat loves — treats and games — to use as rewards later.

The Steps, and When to Move On

These follow the FelineVMA's step-by-step guide. Stay at each step until every cat is comfortable.

  1. Transition room. Bring the new cat in a towel-covered carrier straight to a quiet, latched room with its own food, water, litter box, perches, hiding spots and scratching surfaces. Let it come out on its own. Move on when: It stops hiding constantly and explores confidently — a few days or longer.
  2. Scent swapping. Swap bedding or soft toys between the cats daily. After a few calm days, rub a soft cloth on each cat's cheeks and chin and leave it where the others can smell it. Move on when: No cat shows tension toward the other's scent: no hissing, growling, pacing, avoiding the item or toileting outside the box.
  3. Play on either side of a closed door. Tie two toys together with string and play with one cat on each side of the door, 5–20 minutes at a time. Move on when: All cats play calmly by the door. If a cat hisses or paws at the door, stop and add a barrier.
  4. Supervised exploration. Shut the resident cats in a favourite room with treats, and let the new cat explore the common areas briefly, a little longer each day. Then swap back and let the residents choose whether to come out. Move on when: The new cat explores calmly, and the residents return to their spaces without stress.
  5. Seeing each other through a barrier. Short sessions through a screen, glass door or tall gate, or with one cat in a partly covered carrier. Pair them with play or treats. Move on when: Everyone stays relaxed. If any cat becomes uncomfortable, stop and go back to the door and exploration steps for a few more days.
  6. Supervised time together. Short visits in a large room, on harnesses and leashes or with one cat in a partly covered carrier, rewarding calm behaviour. Keep a cushion or towel handy to block a stare. Move on when: Repeat over several days, gradually longer, until the cats share space without tension.

Managing Tension

  • Check the resources first. The FelineVMA calls too few or visually crowded resources the most common cause of tension.
  • Burn off energy: at least two play sessions a day, of at least 5 minutes each, for high-energy cats.
  • Never pick up cats mid-standoff. Break eye contact with a cushion or toy, then redirect them into separate play or rooms.
  • Block staring with furniture, or give cats shelves and other vertical space.
  • If aggression happens, UC Davis advises going back to the beginning, and talking to your vet if progress stalls.

Bringing Home Two Cats

Cats who are already bonded can share a transition room and work through the steps together. Unbonded cats each need their own room and should be introduced separately — to the residents and to each other — or brought home at different times (FelineVMA).

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to introduce two cats?

The Feline Veterinary Medical Association says introductions can take a few days to a few months, sometimes longer, and UC Davis gives the same range. Don't move to the next step until every cat is comfortable with the current one.

What are signs the introduction is going well?

Every cat eats, drinks and uses its litter box normally, explores confidently, and reacts calmly to the other cat's scent, sounds and, later, sight. Each session should be a little less tense than the last.

What are warning signs that mean I should slow down?

The FelineVMA lists urinating or defecating outside the litter box, avoiding the other cat's scent, growling, hissing or pacing, and other changes in normal behaviour. If any cat becomes uncomfortable, stop and go back to the last calm step for a few more days.

What if my cats fight during the introduction?

Don't pick them up. Put a cushion or toy between them to break eye contact, then separate them into different rooms. UC Davis advises restarting the introduction from the beginning if aggression occurs, and talking to your vet if progress stalls.

How many litter boxes do I need when adding a cat?

One per cat plus one, according to the FelineVMA — and the same for food bowls, water bowls, high-sided beds and scratching posts — spread around the home rather than side by side. Too few or crowded resources are the most common cause of tension.
