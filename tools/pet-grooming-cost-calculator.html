Where the Numbers Come From

The base prices are from CareCredit's November 2025 guide to dog grooming costs, which lists starting prices at a national retail chain with in-store salons: about $30 for a bath, $12 each for a nail trim, ear cleaning, teeth brushing or anal gland expression, $10 for dematting and $22 and up for applying flea and tick treatment. The guide notes that larger dogs usually cost more to groom than small dogs with similar coats, because they take more time and product, and that location matters too.

The default for home supplies, $28 a year, is the ASPCA's 2021 figure for grooming supplies; its first-year table adds $40 for a dog's grooming tools and $20 for a cat's. Replace either with what you actually spend.

Why Two Quotes Can Differ

  • Coat condition. A matted coat takes much longer and may have to be clipped short so the skin can dry, which the AKC notes groomers sometimes need to do.
  • What's included. Nails, ears, teeth and anal glands are sometimes bundled and sometimes charged separately.
  • Size and handling. Bigger dogs take more time and product; a dog that's hard to handle may need a longer appointment.
  • Style. A hand-scissored breed trim takes more time and skill than a short all-over clip.

Home Care That Keeps Visits Simple

For double-coated dogs, the American Kennel Club recommends brushing at least once a week with a slicker brush followed by a comb, brushing the hair rather than the skin, and bathing only when the dog is dirty or smelly, because overbathing strips natural oils. Mats form when the undercoat isn't brushed, and in humid climates they hold moisture against the skin.

Shaving a double coat is generally not advised, according to the AKC, unless tangles stop the skin from drying: it doesn't stop shedding, can damage the topcoat, and raises the risk of skin problems and sunburn, because the undercoat insulates against heat as well as cold.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does dog grooming cost?

It depends on size, coat, condition and the shop, so a local quote is the only reliable figure. As a reference, CareCredit's November 2025 guide lists base prices at a national retail chain of about $30 for a bath and $12 each for a nail trim, ear cleaning, teeth brushing or anal gland expression. A full haircut for a long or curly coat costs more and is priced by the groomer.

How often should I get my dog professionally groomed?

There's no single interval. The American Kennel Club says it depends on how well the coat is kept up at home, the coat type and the climate; dogs with unbrushed coats or in humid areas may need more frequent visits. It recommends brushing double-coated dogs at least once a week and bathing only when a dog is dirty or smelly.

Should I shave my double-coated dog in summer?

Generally no. The AKC advises against shaving a double coat unless tangles and buildup stop the skin from drying. Shaving doesn't stop shedding, can damage the topcoat, and raises the risk of skin problems and sunburn; the undercoat insulates against both cold and heat. Regular brushing is the better way to manage it.
