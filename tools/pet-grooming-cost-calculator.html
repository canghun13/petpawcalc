Why Coat Type Matters More Than Size

Size affects grooming cost, but coat type usually drives the bigger swing. A large short-coated dog like a Labrador is often cheaper to groom than a much smaller long-coated dog like a Shih Tzu, because coat maintenance — detangling, scissor work, de-matting — takes far more time than a quick bath and brush-out on a short coat. Curly and double coats (Poodles, Doodles, Huskies) are the most labor-intensive category, since they mat easily and require specific tools and techniques to groom safely.

Matting specifically can add a real surcharge on top of the base price — light matting around ears or armpits might add $15–$25, while heavy pelted matting requiring a full shave-down can add $35–$50 or more, since it takes significantly longer and is harder on the groomer's equipment.

Shop vs. Mobile Grooming

Mobile grooming — where a groomer comes to you in a self-contained van — typically costs 20–60% more than the same service at a shop, since the groomer owns and operates expensive equipment and loses time driving between appointments. It's often worth the premium for senior or anxious pets who find car rides and shared waiting areas stressful, or for multi-pet households where the time savings offset the higher per-visit cost.

Professional Grooming vs. DIY

At-home grooming supplies (shampoo, slicker brush, nail clippers, de-shedding tools) typically cost $120–$360 per year depending on coat type, which is meaningfully cheaper than professional visits over a full year. The trade-off is time, skill, and the risk of injury from nail trims or matting removal done incorrectly — many owners land on a hybrid approach: professional grooming every 8–12 weeks paired with home maintenance brushing in between.

Skipping grooming entirely to save money often backfires financially — severe matting, overgrown nails, and untreated skin issues from poor coat maintenance frequently cost more in vet bills than consistent grooming would have cost in the first place.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does dog grooming cost?

A single professional grooming session typically costs $40–$80 for small dogs, $50–$110 for medium dogs, $65–$150 for large dogs, and $80–$220 for giant breeds, depending on coat type and service level. Long or curly coats (Poodles, Doodles) can add 20–40% to these prices.

How much does cat grooming cost?

Professional cat grooming typically costs $30–$70 per session for short-haired cats and $50–$100 for long-haired breeds, since most cats need grooming far less often than dogs — many short-haired cats never need a professional groomer at all.

How often should I get my dog professionally groomed?

Short-coated dogs typically need professional grooming every 8–12 weeks. Medium coats do well every 6–8 weeks. Long, curly, or double coats — especially Poodles, Doodles, and similar breeds — often need grooming every 4–6 weeks to prevent matting.
