What Moves Your Quote

  • Age. Insurers usually reprice at renewal and age is one of the factors; some set a maximum age for new enrollment.
  • Breed and size, because insurers price in the conditions a breed is prone to.
  • Where you live, because vet prices differ by area.
  • Deductible and reimbursement rate. A higher deductible lowers the premium; a higher reimbursement percentage raises it.
  • What's covered. Accident-only plans are cheapest; wellness cover for routine care costs extra.

What Insurance Is For

Accident-and-illness cover is for the bills you can't predict. Approximate costs at emergency hospitals, compiled by Preventive Vet, include $600–$1,700 for one to two days in hospital, $1,500–$5,000 for emergency surgery and $2,000–$7,500 or more for bloat (GDV). Most policies exclude pre-existing conditions — anything noted in your pet's records before cover starts — which is why the timing of enrollment matters as much as the price.

Waiting periods before cover begins vary by insurer and condition, and some policies treat orthopedic conditions such as cruciate ligament injuries differently. Read the policy's waiting-period and exclusions sections before buying; our waiting period tracker turns your policy's terms into dates.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does pet insurance cost per month?

According to the North American Pet Health Insurance Association, the average US accident-and-illness premium in 2025 was $836 a year for dogs (about $70 a month) and $435 for cats (about $36 a month). Accident-only cover averaged $190 and $112 a year. Your quote will depend on your pet's age, breed, where you live and the deductible and reimbursement rate you choose.

Is the estimate the same as a quote?

No. The averages are national figures across all ages, breeds and plan settings. Real quotes are often well above or below them, so use the average to get a sense of scale and get quotes from several insurers for your pet.

Is pet insurance worth it?

It depends on your pet and how large a bill you could pay from savings. Insurance pays off in the years a pet has a major accident or illness — Preventive Vet's ranges put emergency surgery at $1,500–$5,000 and bloat (GDV) at $2,000–$7,500 or more — and costs more than it returns in years when it doesn't. Most policies exclude pre-existing conditions, so cover bought after a problem appears won't pay for that problem.

Are pet insurance premiums going up?

Yes, on average. NAPHIA reported that the average accident-and-illness premium rose 11.5% for dogs and 12.6% for cats from 2024 to 2025. Individual policies are also usually repriced at renewal, with age one of the factors.
