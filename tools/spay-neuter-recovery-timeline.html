🩹 Spay/Neuter Recovery Timeline

⚕️ This calendar follows the ASPCA Spay/Neuter Alliance's general aftercare instructions. Your own clinic's discharge instructions come first, especially after a more complex surgery (pregnancy, uterine infection or retained testicles).

The ASPCA's Recovery Rules

  • Rest for 7–10 days. No running, jumping on or off furniture, rough play or long walks. Confine your pet when you can't supervise, carry small pets on stairs, and use a leash for bathroom trips. Keep cats in a quiet room with food, water and a clean litter box.
  • No baths or ointments for 10 days — wet skin glue dissolves too quickly.
  • Check the incision at least twice a day.
  • Food: a half-size meal when you get home, then the normal evening meal. Appetite should return within 24 hours. Puppies and kittens may have an extra meal during the day.
  • Licking: stop any licking or chewing at the incision with an e-collar, and put it straight back on after meals.
  • Stitches: females have internal stitches that dissolve and skin glue; male cats have no stitches. If skin stitches or staples were used, they come out at a recheck at about 10 days.
  • Pain medication: call the clinic if your pet seems in pain. Never give human medication — it can be fatal.

What the Incision Should Look Like

  • Females (dogs and cats): one incision in the middle of the belly, with no drainage or odor and minimal redness and swelling.
  • Male dogs: one incision on the scrotum; a small amount of drainage can continue for up to three days.
  • Male cats: two small incisions, one on each side of the scrotum. He may still look as if he has testicles; the swelling should go down gradually.

Mild redness and swelling should settle within several days, and bumps or bruises should shrink. In females, a little blood in the urine is normal in the first 24 hours.

Call the Clinic Right Away If You See

  • pale gums, depression or unsteady walking
  • loss of appetite or drinking less, or lethargy lasting more than 24 hours
  • vomiting or diarrhea
  • discharge or bleeding from the incision
  • trouble urinating or defecating, or blood in the urine that continues or appears later
  • labored breathing

Keeping Pets Apart

The ASPCA notes that a newly neutered male can still get an unspayed female pregnant for up to 30 days, and that spayed females should be kept away from unneutered males for seven days. Pets coming home from the clinic may smell different to housemates, so keep them apart for a few days if there's any tension.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should my pet rest after a spay or neuter?

The ASPCA's aftercare instructions call for limiting running, jumping and play for 7–10 days, confining your pet when you can't supervise, carrying small pets on stairs and using a leash for bathroom trips.

When can my pet have a bath after surgery?

Not for 10 days, according to the ASPCA, and no ointments either: wet skin glue dissolves too quickly.

Does my pet need a cone after a spay or neuter?

Use an e-collar if your pet licks or chews at the incision, the ASPCA says, and put it straight back on after meals. If your pet can still reach the incision, get a larger collar or call the clinic.

When do stitches come out?

Often they don't need to. The ASPCA notes females have internal stitches that dissolve plus skin glue, and male cats have no stitches. If skin stitches or staples were used, they come out at a recheck at about 10 days.

What's normal and what needs a call after surgery?

Mild redness and swelling that settles within several days is normal, and male dogs can have slight drainage for up to three days. Call the clinic for pale gums, lethargy over 24 hours, loss of appetite, vomiting or diarrhea, discharge or bleeding from the incision, trouble urinating, or labored breathing.
