Core vs. Non-Core Dog Vaccines

Core vaccines are recommended for every dog regardless of lifestyle, because the diseases they prevent are common, severe, or spread easily. These are DHPP (distemper, hepatitis/adenovirus, parvovirus, parainfluenza) and rabies, which is legally required in most U.S. states.

Non-core vaccines are given based on real exposure risk — not just whether a dog "goes outside." Bordetella (kennel cough) matters for dogs in boarding, daycare, or grooming settings. Leptospirosis matters for dogs with exposure to wildlife, standing water, or rural areas. Lyme disease vaccine matters in tick-heavy regions. Canine influenza matters for dogs in high-density social settings.

Why Puppies Need Multiple Rounds

Antibodies from the mother protect puppies for a while but can also stop a vaccine from working, and they fade at different ages in different puppies. That's why the AAHA guidelines repeat the core vaccine every 2–4 weeks until 16 weeks.

The 2022 AAHA Canine Vaccination Guidelines call for at least three doses between 6 and 16 weeks, 2–4 weeks apart, then a booster within a year of the last puppy dose.

Adult Booster Schedules

After the puppy series and a one-year booster, many vets move core vaccines (DHPP) to a 3-year schedule for healthy adult dogs, based on research showing extended immunity. Rabies boosters follow local law — often every 1 or 3 years depending on the vaccine type and jurisdiction. Non-core vaccines are typically re-evaluated annually based on lifestyle, since exposure risk can change year to year.

Titer testing — a blood test measuring existing antibody levels — is an option some owners use to check whether a booster is actually needed before automatically re-vaccinating. It's not available or appropriate for every vaccine (notably not a substitute for legally required rabies boosters), so it's worth discussing with your vet rather than assuming it applies across the board.

Why Deworming Takes More Than One Dose

CAPC recommends deworming puppies from 2 weeks of age and repeating it every 2 weeks until regular broad-spectrum parasite control begins. Where year-round control isn't used, its schedule is every 2 weeks until 2 months, monthly until 6 months, then four times a year.

Puppies can be born already infected: the Merck Veterinary Manual says dormant roundworm larvae in a pregnant dog move into the puppies, its most common route of infection, and larvae also pass in milk. Roundworms can be in a puppy's intestine as early as a week after birth, which is why CAPC starts deworming at 2 weeks.

Roundworms and Human Health

Dog roundworms can infect people. Most infections cause no symptoms, but the Merck Veterinary Manual describes visceral larva migrans and, rarely, eye damage, and advises strict hygiene, especially for children. Deworming on schedule and picking up feces promptly reduce the risk.

Pair Deworming With Fecal Exams

CAPC recommends fecal exams at least four times in a puppy's first year and at least twice a year for healthy adults, adjusted for lifestyle. Deworming treats; the fecal exam shows what's actually present. Your vet chooses the product and dose.

Frequently Asked Questions

What vaccines does a dog need every year?

Core vaccines don't need to be yearly for most adults: after the first-year booster, AAHA recommends them every 3 years. Rabies follows local law (1- or 3-year vaccines). Lifestyle vaccines such as leptospirosis, Bordetella, canine influenza and Lyme are yearly if your dog needs them.

At what age do puppies start vaccines?

The 2022 AAHA Canine Vaccination Guidelines recommend at least three doses of the core distemper–adenovirus–parvovirus vaccine between 6 and 16 weeks, 2–4 weeks apart, then a booster within a year of the last puppy dose.

Does my indoor dog need non-core vaccines?

It depends on exposure. AAHA recommends Bordetella and influenza based on lifestyle (boarding, daycare, dog parks) and Lyme in tick areas, and considers most dogs in North America at risk of leptospirosis. Ask your vet to review your dog's risk each year.

How often should puppies be dewormed?

CAPC recommends starting at 2 weeks of age and repeating every 2 weeks until regular broad-spectrum parasite control begins. Without year-round control, its schedule is every 2 weeks until 2 months, monthly until 6 months, then four times a year.

Why can't one deworming treatment finish the job?

Puppies are usually infected before birth and can be re-exposed through milk and their surroundings, so one dose doesn't cover the risk period. That's why CAPC repeats treatment every 2 weeks early on and recommends fecal exams at least four times in the first year.

Can worms in dogs spread to humans?

Yes. The Merck Veterinary Manual says most human roundworm infections cause no symptoms but can cause visceral larva migrans and, rarely, eye damage, and advises strict hygiene, especially for children. Regular deworming and prompt cleanup both help.

Do adult dogs still need regular deworming?

CAPC recommends year-round broad-spectrum parasite control covering heartworm, intestinal parasites, fleas and ticks; without it, a broad-spectrum dewormer four times a year. Healthy adults should also have fecal exams at least twice a year.

Can I determine my dog's exact dewormer dosage from an online calculator?

No — dosage depends on your dog's weight, the specific product, and your vet's judgment about your dog's individual parasite risk, so it should always come from your veterinarian rather than a general online tool. This calculator covers timing and intervals only.
