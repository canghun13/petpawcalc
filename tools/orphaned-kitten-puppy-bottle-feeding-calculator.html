🐾 PetPawCalc
⚕️ Hand-rearing a newborn is veterinary-level care. Any orphaned kitten or puppy should see a vet to check weight, hydration and the age estimate the plan depends on. Follow the mixing instructions on your milk replacer.

Warm First. Always.

The National Kitten Coalition is blunt: never feed a cold kitten. Hypothermia stops food moving through the gut, so feeding a chilled kitten can be fatal; VCA likewise says puppies won't eat well when hypothermic. Kittens don't develop a shivering reflex until about 2 weeks and can't regulate their body temperature until about 4 weeks, so they can't recover on their own.

The Coalition's warming advice:

  • Start warming straight away with consistent, moderate heat over one to three hours; rewarming too fast causes its own problems.
  • Use a covered warm water bottle, heated rice bag, or pet-safe heating pad on low. Nothing should touch the skin directly, and the kitten must be able to move away from the heat.
  • No heat lamps — they can dehydrate or burn kittens.
  • Holding the kitten against your body only partly helps, because your temperature is lower than a kitten's.
  • Take every chilled kitten to a vet or emergency clinic, even if it responds to warming.

Kittens: How Much and How Often

Shelter feeding charts adapted from Maddie's Fund give a daily total of about 27 ml of kitten milk replacer per 100 g of body weight (27 ml for a 100 g kitten, 54 ml at 200 g), and a comfortable stomach capacity of about 4 ml per 100 g. Overfeeding can cause diarrhea, regurgitation and aspiration. The ASPCA's bottle-feeding guide adds that kittens can eat until satisfied but shouldn't take more than double their stomach capacity at one feeding.

Puppies: Calories, Then Volume

VCA Hospitals gives orphaned puppies' needs as calories rather than millilitres: about 15 kcal per 100 g of body weight a day on days 1–3, 20 kcal on days 4–6, and up to 25 kcal after day 6. Because milk replacers differ, the calculator divides that by the calories per ml on your formula's label. VCA says to feed every 2–4 hours in the first week, then every 4–6 hours until weaning, and to warm the formula to about 100°F (38°C) — cold formula can cause regurgitation, aspiration, bloating and diarrhea.

Puppies should gain weight every day and, on average, double their birth weight by day 10 (VCA).

Nest Temperature

The National Kitten Coalition's ambient nest temperatures for kittens: 85–90°F at 0–1 weeks, 79–84°F at 2–3 weeks, and 73.5–79°F at 4 weeks, with room humidity of 55–60%. Normal rectal temperature is lower in newborns: about 95–99°F at 0–1 weeks, 97–100°F at 2–3 weeks and 99–101°F at 4 weeks.

Weight Is the Feedback

Weigh at the same time every day on a gram scale and write down how much was taken at each feed. Cats Protection gives a healthy kitten's gain as about 10–15 g a day. A kitten or puppy that stops gaining or loses weight needs a vet.

Not Formula

Cow's milk isn't a substitute. VCA notes a mother dog's milk has more than twice the protein of cow's milk, which is also lower in calories and fat and higher in lactose, and Today's Veterinary Nurse calls cow's milk a poor taurine source for kittens. Use a commercial kitten or puppy milk replacer and follow its label.

After Every Feed: Stimulation

Newborns can't urinate or defecate on their own. After each feed, gently rub the genital area with a warm, damp cloth or cotton ball until they go — for kittens until about 4 weeks (Pasadena Humane), and for puppies until about 3 weeks (VCA). The ASPCA's guide says kittens should urinate at every feeding and pass stool about once a day.

Weaning

Kittens can be offered wet kitten food in a shallow dish at about 4 weeks and weaned gradually from 5 weeks (Pasadena Humane); Today's Veterinary Nurse puts weaning at 3–4 weeks to start and 6–9 weeks to finish. VCA says puppies can start weaning at 3–4 weeks and should be on solid food by 5–6 weeks.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much formula does an orphaned kitten need?

Shelter feeding charts adapted from Maddie's Fund give about 27 ml of kitten milk replacer per 100 g of body weight a day, split across the day's feeds. A 150 g kitten needs about 40 ml in 24 hours. Weigh daily and recalculate as the kitten grows.

Why can't I feed a cold kitten?

The National Kitten Coalition says hypothermia stops food moving through the gut, so feeding a chilled kitten can be fatal. Warm it with consistent, moderate heat over one to three hours, then feed, and take it to a vet even if it responds.

How often should I bottle feed a newborn kitten?

Pasadena Humane's chart, adapted from Kitten Lady, gives every 2 hours in the first week, every 2–3 hours at 1–2 weeks, every 3–4 hours at 2–3 weeks, every 4–5 hours at 3–4 weeks and every 5–6 hours at 4–5 weeks. Some shelter charts list 6–7 feeds a day for the smallest kittens.

How much formula does an orphaned puppy need?

VCA Hospitals gives about 15 kcal per 100 g of body weight a day on days 1–3, 20 kcal on days 4–6 and up to 25 kcal after day 6, fed every 2–4 hours in the first week and every 4–6 hours after. Divide by the calories per ml on your milk replacer's label to get the volume.

Can I give a kitten or puppy cow's milk?

No. VCA notes a mother dog's milk has more than twice the protein of cow's milk, which is also lower in calories and fat and higher in lactose, and Today's Veterinary Nurse calls it a poor taurine source for kittens. Use a commercial kitten or puppy milk replacer.

How do I know if the kitten is getting enough?

Weight. Cats Protection gives a healthy kitten's gain as about 10–15 g a day; VCA says puppies should gain daily and double their birth weight by about day 10. Weigh at the same time each day on a gram scale, and see a vet if gain stops or weight drops.
