Contamination Audit — Do This Before Day One

Trials rarely fail because the diet was wrong. They fail because something else got into the pet. Tick everything you have actually dealt with — anything left unticked is a hole in the trial.

⚕️ This planner organises a protocol your veterinarian prescribes — it does not replace them. The trial diet itself must be chosen by your vet, since suitable diets are hydrolyzed or genuinely novel for your individual pet and most are prescription-only. Do not stop or change prescribed medication to run a trial, and never withhold food from a cat that refuses the new diet.

Why the Trial Has to Be This Strict

A food allergy is an immune reaction to a specific protein. The only way to prove one exists is to remove every protein the immune system already knows, wait long enough for the reaction to settle, and then see whether putting the old food back brings the signs straight back. There is no shortcut around that, which is why the protocol is so unforgiving: a single flavored chew in week six can restart the clock without you ever knowing it happened.

The duration comes from the evidence. A 2015 critically appraised topic by Olivry, Mueller and Prélaud in BMC Veterinary Research pooled the published trials: in dogs, about half had a marked improvement by 3 weeks, over 85% had returned to normal by 5 weeks and over 95% by 8 weeks; in cats, about 80% were in remission by 6 weeks and about 90% by 8 weeks. The authors concluded that extending a trial to 8 weeks raises its sensitivity to over 90% in both species. Stop at week four and a pet that would have responded can look like a failure.

Food Allergy Is Less Common Than the Internet Suggests

Food is one item on a longer list of causes of itching, alongside environmental allergies, fleas and skin infections, so your vet will usually rule out fleas and infection first. A diet trial is how food is confirmed or ruled out; the signs alone can't tell you. In dogs with recurring ear infections, food allergy is worth considering: in a study cited by veterinary dermatologist Melissa Hall in dvm360, 55% of 65 food-allergic dogs had otitis externa.

Tracking Whether It's Actually Working

Improvement over eight weeks is gradual, and you see your pet every day, so it's hard to judge from memory. Scoring the itch on a fixed scale at the start and then weekly gives you a comparison that memory cannot: our Pet Itch Score Tracker uses the owner-assessed pruritus scale validated for exactly this purpose, and the planner's recheck dates are the points at which to record a score.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an elimination diet trial take?

Eight weeks on the trial diet, plus a week of transition. A 2015 critically appraised topic by Olivry, Mueller and Prélaud found over 95% of food-allergic dogs had returned to normal by 8 weeks and about 90% of cats were in remission, and concluded that 8 weeks raises the trial's sensitivity to over 90% in both species.

How long does a food trial take for a cat?

The same 8 weeks: in the 2015 review, about 80% of food-allergic cats were in remission by 6 weeks and about 90% by 8. Never starve a cat into accepting a trial diet; if your cat refuses the food for more than a day or two, call your vet, because going without food risks hepatic lipidosis.

Why do most elimination diet trials fail?

Contamination, not the diet. The trial only works if the prescribed food and water are the only things your pet eats. The usual leaks are flavored: chewable heartworm and flea preventives, flavored medications, dental chews, toothpaste, pill pockets, treats, table scraps and other pets' food.

Are blood or saliva tests for food allergies accurate?

No. A 2017 critically appraised topic by Mueller and Olivry found serum food-specific IgE and IgG tests had low repeatability and highly variable accuracy in dogs, low accuracy in cats, and that other tests, including saliva and hair, had only small single studies behind them. The elimination diet trial followed by a challenge remains the way to diagnose a food allergy.

What is the challenge phase, and do I have to do it?

After a successful trial you reintroduce the original food and watch for signs to return. Without it you only know your pet improved on a new diet, not why — the season, flea control or medication could be the reason. A flare confirms a food reaction. Your vet decides whether to rechallenge and how long to watch.

Can I use a grain-free or limited-ingredient store food for the trial?

Your vet should choose the trial diet. Trial diets are either hydrolyzed — proteins broken into fragments too small to trigger a reaction — or built on proteins your pet has never eaten. Over-the-counter limited-ingredient foods can contain proteins not prominent on the label, and grain-free doesn't address protein allergy.
