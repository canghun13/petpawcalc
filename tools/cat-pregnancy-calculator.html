Week-by-Week Guide

⚕️ The due date counts 63–65 days from mating, the average VCA Hospitals gives for cats, inside a normal range of about 60–67 days. Some cats go as long as 71 days. Work with your veterinarian for prenatal care.

How Long Are Cats Pregnant?

VCA Hospitals gives cat gestation as about 60–67 days, averaging 63–65, and notes that most cats give birth between days 63 and 65, with some going as long as 71 days. Because cats ovulate in response to mating, the mating date is a good starting point for the count. If the pregnancy goes past about 71 days from a known mating date, call your vet.

PetMD divides a cat's pregnancy into three trimesters of about 20 days each. Embryos implant at about day 14, according to the Merck Veterinary Manual.

Signs Your Cat May Be Pregnant

  • "Pinking up" — pinker, more prominent nipples — around days 16–20
  • A slight increase in appetite in the first few weeks
  • A growing belly, usually from the second trimester
  • Nesting in the final week

False pregnancy is rare in cats, but PetMD notes an affected cat can still develop mammary tissue and even produce milk, so a vet exam is the way to be sure.

Pregnancy Confirmation and Diagnosis: Methods Compared

Timings are from PetMD and the Merck Veterinary Manual.

Method Timing What it tells you
Gentle abdominal palpation by a vet About 2½ weeks Can detect the developing kittens; needs a relaxed cat and an experienced hand
Ultrasound Heartbeats at about 3–4 weeks Confirms live kittens and estimates how far along she is; Merck notes scans before day 21 can miss a pregnancy
X-ray From about day 45 (6 weeks) Kittens' skeletons are visible once calcified; the usual way to count them

Knowing how many kittens to expect tells you when the birth is finished.

What to Prepare

Food: kittens grow throughout pregnancy, so a cat's needs rise from early on — unlike a dog's. VCA recommends a kitten or growth food, ideally one that has passed an AAFCO feeding trial for gestation and lactation or all life stages. Merck notes intake typically rises about 25% on average in the last third, and VCA recommends several small meals in the last trimester.

Nesting box: VCA suggests a quiet box with low sides, lined with several layers of disposable absorbent material, introduced before the final week.

Signs labor is close: milk may appear 24–48 hours before labor; in the last 24 hours her rectal temperature typically falls below 100°F (37.8°C) and she usually stops eating.

When to Call the Vet

According to VCA, get help for 20 minutes of intense straining without a kitten, a kitten or fluid bubble at the vulva that isn't delivered within a few minutes, fresh blood from the vulva for more than 10 minutes, a rectal temperature above 103°F, sudden lethargy, or labor that stops and doesn't restart within a few hours of the first kittens or for 24 hours or more. Merck adds dark green or foul-smelling discharge before the first kitten.

Kittens usually arrive 10 minutes to an hour apart. Some cats deliver a few kittens, rest for 24–36 hours while nursing them, and then deliver the rest — VCA describes this interrupted labor as normal. The difference that matters is whether she is straining.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long are cats pregnant?

About 63–65 days on average. VCA Hospitals gives a normal range of about 60–67 days and notes that most cats give birth between days 63 and 65, though some go as long as 71 days.

How do I calculate my cat's due date?

Count 63–65 days from the mating date. Cats ovulate in response to mating, so the mating date is a good starting point. If she had access to a tom over several days, count from the first and last possible dates. If you don't know the date, a vet can estimate it by ultrasound or X-ray.

What are the signs that a cat is about to give birth?

Nesting usually starts in the final week. Milk may appear 24–48 hours before labor. In the last 24 hours her rectal temperature typically falls below 100°F (37.8°C) and she usually stops eating.

How can I confirm my cat is pregnant?

A vet may feel the developing kittens at about two and a half weeks. Heartbeats can be seen on ultrasound at about three to four weeks, though Merck notes scans before day 21 can miss a pregnancy. X-rays show the skeletons from about day 45 and are the usual way to count kittens.
