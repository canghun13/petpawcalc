Week-by-Week Guide

⚕️ This calculator provides estimates based on average canine gestation (63 days). Actual delivery may vary by ±5 days. Always work with your veterinarian for accurate pregnancy monitoring.

How Long Are Dogs Pregnant?

Dog pregnancy (gestation) lasts an average of 63 days from the mating date, though the normal range is 58–68 days. Smaller breeds tend to deliver a little earlier; giant breeds a little later.

The pregnancy is divided into three trimesters of roughly 21 days each. Each trimester brings visible changes — from early behavioral shifts to a noticeably rounded belly by week 5, and active nesting behavior in the final weeks.

Signs Your Dog May Be Pregnant

  • Decreased appetite or mild nausea in weeks 1–3
  • Nipple enlargement and darkening (week 3–4)
  • Increased affection or clinginess
  • Visible belly rounding by week 5
  • Nesting behavior and restlessness in the final week

Pregnancy Confirmation and Diagnosis: Methods Compared

Physical signs alone are not a reliable pregnancy check — a false pregnancy (pseudopregnancy) can produce nearly identical symptoms without any puppies. If you need a real confirmation rather than a guess, here's how the main diagnostic methods compare:

Method Earliest reliable timing Accuracy What it tells you
Relaxin blood test Day 21–25 High — relaxin is only produced during true pregnancy, so this rules out pseudopregnancy Pregnant or not (not litter size)
Abdominal palpation Day 21–28 Moderate — depends heavily on the vet's experience and the dog's size/tension Rough sense of pregnancy, not a reliable count
Ultrasound Day 25–35 High — the most reliable early confirmation, and can detect fetal heartbeats Confirms pregnancy and viability (not an exact puppy count)
X-ray Day 45+ (skeletons must be calcified) High for counting — the only reliable way to count puppies Exact puppy count, useful for whelping planning

In practice, most vets confirm with ultrasound around week 4 and follow up with an X-ray around week 7 to get an exact count — knowing the number of puppies in advance helps you recognize when delivery is complete.

What to Prepare

Around week 7, set up a whelping box — a quiet, warm, easy-to-clean space where your dog can give birth. Introduce her to it early so she feels comfortable.

Schedule vet checkups at week 4 (ultrasound to confirm pregnancy) and week 7–8 (X-ray to count puppies). Have your vet's emergency number ready for the final week.

When to Call the Vet Immediately

Most canine pregnancies proceed without complications, but certain signs warrant an immediate call: green or bloody discharge before labor begins, active straining for more than 30 minutes without a puppy delivered, more than 2–4 hours between puppies with visible distress, or a temperature drop below 99°F that doesn't lead to labor within 24 hours.

First-time mothers and small-breed dogs (especially brachycephalic breeds like French Bulldogs) have a higher likelihood of needing veterinary assistance during delivery. If your dog falls into either category, discuss a delivery plan with your vet well before the due date.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long are dogs pregnant?

Dog pregnancy lasts approximately 63 days from ovulation. Because mating doesn't always coincide precisely with ovulation, the actual duration from a specific mating date can range from about 58 to 68 days.

How do I calculate my dog's due date?

Count approximately 63 days from the mating date. This is an estimate — actual delivery can occur several days earlier or later. A veterinarian can provide a more precise estimate using ultrasound or progesterone testing.

What are the signs that a dog is about to give birth?

Within 24–48 hours of labor, most dogs become restless, stop eating, and may pant or seek isolation. A rectal temperature drop below 100°F (37.8°C) is a reliable sign that labor is typically within 12–24 hours.

How can I confirm or diagnose my dog's pregnancy?

A relaxin hormone blood test can confirm pregnancy from around day 21–25 and rules out false pregnancy — this blood test is the most common method for dog pregnancy confirmation early on. Ultrasound (day 25–35) is the most reliable form of dog pregnancy diagnosis and can detect heartbeats. An X-ray after day 45, once puppy skeletons have calcified, is the only reliable way to get an exact puppy count. Whichever method you use, a proper dog pregnancy check should always involve your veterinarian rather than guesswork from outward signs alone.

What are the early signs a dog is pregnant?

The early signs of a pregnant dog are subtle and easy to miss. In the first 2–3 weeks, signs and symptoms of a pregnant dog can include a slightly reduced appetite or brief bout of nausea, a drop in energy, and mild behavioral clinginess. Nipple enlargement and darkening usually appears around week 3–4. Pregnant dog symptoms that most owners notice — visible abdominal swelling and clear weight gain — generally do not show until week 5 onward, which is why dog signs of pregnancy symptoms alone are not a reliable way to confirm anything early.

When can a vet tell if a dog is pregnant?

When can a vet tell if a dog is pregnant depends on the method. Abdominal palpation works roughly days 21–35, when the fetal swellings are still distinct enough to feel. A relaxin blood test is generally reliable from about day 22–25. Ultrasound from around day 25–28 confirms pregnancy and fetal heartbeats but does not give a reliable puppy count. X-ray from about day 45–55, once the skeletons have mineralized, is the most accurate way to count puppies before whelping.
