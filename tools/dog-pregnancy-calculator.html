Enter your dog's mating date to get her estimated due date and a week-by-week guide to what's happening — and what to prepare.

Week-by-Week Guide

⚕️ The due date counts 63 days forward. From a mating date the normal window is 63 days give or take 7; from an ovulation date confirmed by progesterone testing it is 63 days give or take 1–2 (Cornell Riney Canine Health Center). Work with your veterinarian for monitoring and whelping plans.

How Long Are Dogs Pregnant?

According to Cornell's Riney Canine Health Center, dogs are pregnant for about 63 days from ovulation. Mating isn't the same as conception: sperm can survive for days and the eggs need 2–3 days to mature after ovulation, so from a mating date Cornell gives 63 days give or take 7. If your vet timed ovulation with progesterone blood tests, the window narrows to 63 days give or take 1–2. With two matings, VCA suggests expecting puppies 63–65 days after the later one.

Very little is visible in the first month. Weight and belly size usually start to rise in the second month, and mammary development and nesting come in the last weeks.

Signs Your Dog May Be Pregnant

  • First month: usually nothing you can rely on
  • Second month: weight starts to rise and the belly may begin to fill out
  • Last weeks: clearly larger belly, mammary development, sometimes milk; nesting and restlessness in the final days

A false pregnancy after a heat can produce almost the same signs — VCA notes that most intact females show some of them — so only a vet test confirms a real pregnancy.

Pregnancy Confirmation and Diagnosis: Methods Compared

Here's how the main ways a vet confirms pregnancy compare. Timings are from the Merck Veterinary Manual and VCA Hospitals.

Method Earliest reliable timing Accuracy What it tells you
Relaxin blood test After about day 22–27 Can be falsely negative if done too early or with a very small litter; relaxin is not produced in a false pregnancy Pregnant or not (not litter size)
Abdominal palpation Around day 21–28 Depends on a relaxed dog; less reliable after days 35–38 Rough sense of pregnancy, not a reliable count
Ultrasound From about 3 weeks; reliable after day 28 VCA's preferred method for confirming pregnancy Confirms pregnancy; counting puppies is difficult
X-ray From about day 42–45; best after day 55 Skeletons must have mineralized first The most accurate puppy count, useful for knowing when whelping is finished

A common plan is an ultrasound after day 28 to confirm, then an X-ray after day 55 for the count.

What to Prepare

Set up a whelping area — quiet, warm and easy to clean — in the last few weeks so she is used to it before labor.

Feeding: VCA's guidance is to keep her intake the same for about the first 6 weeks after mating, then increase it gradually with smaller, more frequent meals, up to about 1.5 times her normal amount in the last three weeks. VCA recommends a diet with an AAFCO statement for "gestation and lactation" or "all life stages".

Temperature: normal rectal temperature is about 100–102.5°F. Cornell notes it can fall below 99°F within 24 hours of birth, but the dip lasts only about 8 hours, so twice-daily checks in the last week can miss it.

When to Call the Vet Immediately

Based on Cornell and the Merck Veterinary Manual, call your vet if more than 2 hours pass between puppies, she has strong contractions for more than 30 minutes without producing a puppy, there's dark green or foul-smelling discharge before the first puppy, whelping hasn't started within 24–36 hours of the temperature drop, the pregnancy goes beyond 70 days from a known breeding date, or she seems very unwell, weak or in severe pain.

Merck puts difficult birth (dystocia) at about 5% of pregnancies overall, but it is far more common in some breeds, especially flat-faced and short-legged breeds such as Bulldogs, Pugs, Boston Terriers and Chihuahuas. For those breeds, make a plan with your vet well before the due date.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long are dogs pregnant?

About 63 days from ovulation, according to Cornell's Riney Canine Health Center. From a mating date the window is 63 days give or take 7, because mating isn't the same as conception. If your vet timed ovulation with progesterone tests, it narrows to 63 days give or take 1–2.

How do I calculate my dog's due date?

Count 63 days forward from the date. From a mating date, treat 7 days either side as normal; from a progesterone-timed ovulation date, 1–2 days. With two matings, VCA suggests expecting puppies 63–65 days after the later one.

What are the signs that a dog is about to give birth?

Her rectal temperature often drops below 99°F within 24 hours of birth (normal is about 100–102.5°F), though the dip lasts only about 8 hours and is easy to miss. Nesting, restlessness and panting usually start 6–12 hours before the first puppy.

When can a vet confirm a dog is pregnant, and how?

An ultrasound may detect pregnancy from about 3 weeks after mating and is reliable after day 28. A relaxin blood test works after about day 22–27. Palpation can sometimes find the swellings around day 21–28 in a relaxed dog. X-rays show the skeletons from about day 42–45 and give the best puppy count after day 55.

Should I feed my pregnant dog more?

Not in the first two thirds of pregnancy. VCA's guidance is to keep her intake the same for about the first 6 weeks after mating, then increase it gradually with smaller, more frequent meals, up to about 1.5 times her normal amount in the last three weeks.

When should I call the vet during whelping?

Call if more than 2 hours pass between puppies, she has strong contractions for more than 30 minutes without a puppy, there's dark green or foul-smelling discharge before the first puppy, whelping hasn't started within 24–36 hours of the temperature drop, or the pregnancy goes past 70 days from a known breeding date.
