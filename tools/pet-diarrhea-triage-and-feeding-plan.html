The Fasting Advice Has Been Overturned

"Withhold food for 24 hours" is still the most commonly repeated advice for a pet with diarrhoea, and it reflects thinking that has moved on.

VCA Animal Hospitals puts it directly: fasting pets with gastrointestinal upset for 24 to 48 hours used to be common practice because the gut was thought to need rest, but it is now better understood — in human and veterinary medicine alike — that the gut needs nutrients while it is recovering, and withholding food can cause more damage and delay recovery. PetMD makes the practical version of the same point: if a dog has diarrhoea and is not vomiting, there is no need to withhold food.

Being straight about the disagreement: this is not fully settled. Plenty of veterinary clinics still suggest a short fast of up to 12 hours for a healthy adult dog with mild, uncomplicated diarrhoea, and that is a defensible position. What has changed is the long fast, and the assumption that fasting is the default first move. If your own vet has told you to fast your specific pet, follow them — they know the case.

The Pets Who Should Never Be Fasted

This part was never a matter of fashion. For some animals, going without food is actively dangerous:

  • Cats. Going without food puts cats at risk of hepatic lipidosis, a potentially fatal liver condition. A cat that will not eat is a reason to call a vet, not a situation to wait out.
  • Puppies and kittens. Very little reserve and a real risk of dropping blood sugar. Diarrhoea in a young animal is a same-day call in its own right.
  • Toy and small breeds. Same hypoglycaemia problem, at any age.
  • Seniors, diabetics, and pets on medication or with a diagnosed condition. Skipping meals interacts with the condition and often with the drugs.

If a pet in any of these groups is refusing food on their own, that is a finding to report rather than a fast to manage.

What the Blood Is Telling You

Where blood comes from changes how worried to be, and you can read that from the colour:

AppearanceWhat it suggestsUrgency
Bright red streaks (haematochezia)Bleeding low down — colon or rectum, often colitis or strainingVet appointment; sooner if repeated or the pet is unwell
Large volume of blood, or raspberry-jamAcute haemorrhagic diarrhoea syndrome (AHDS/HGE), most often small and toy breedsEmergency — dehydration and shock can develop within hours
Black, tarry, sticky (melena)Digested blood from the stomach or small intestine — ulcers, toxins, clotting problemsEmergency, always
Mucus or jelly-like coatingLarge bowel irritation; common with colitis, parasites and stressVet appointment, not usually an emergency alone

One caution: bismuth-containing stomach remedies turn stool black. If you have given one, you have removed your own ability to distinguish that from melena — which is a good reason not to give one.

Behaviour Is Not a Reliable Severity Gauge

Dogs frequently look fine in the early stages of conditions like AHDS and pancreatitis. "He's still wagging" is genuinely reassuring for a mild upset and genuinely misleading when there is blood involved. The stool findings and the pet's age carry more weight than demeanour, which is why the tool above asks about both rather than just how they seem.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I fast my dog with diarrhea?

Probably not, and this is guidance that has genuinely changed. VCA Animal Hospitals describes the position plainly: fasting pets with gastrointestinal upset for 24 to 48 hours used to be common practice on the belief that the gut needed rest, but it is now better understood that the gut needs nutrients while it is recovering and that withholding food can cause more damage and delay recovery. PetMD makes a similar point — if a dog has diarrhoea and is not vomiting, there is no need to withhold food. Some clinics still suggest a short fast of up to 12 hours for a healthy adult dog with mild uncomplicated diarrhoea, so opinions have not fully converged, but the direction of travel is clear.

Which pets should never be fasted?

Cats, puppies, kittens, toy and small breeds, seniors, diabetics, and any pet with an existing health condition. For cats the risk is specific and serious: going without food puts them at risk of hepatic lipidosis, a potentially fatal liver condition. Puppies and toy breeds can become hypoglycaemic surprisingly quickly. If a pet in any of these groups will not eat, that is itself a reason to call a vet rather than something to wait out.

When is dog diarrhea an emergency?

Go now, not tomorrow, for: black tarry stool, diarrhoea that looks like raspberry jam, a large amount of blood, diarrhoea in a puppy or unvaccinated dog, diarrhoea with repeated vomiting, pale gums, weakness or collapse, a painful or bloated belly, or a known toxin or swallowed object. Black tarry stool is called melena and means digested blood from higher in the tract; veterinary sources treat it as an emergency without exception. Raspberry-jam diarrhoea suggests acute haemorrhagic diarrhoea syndrome, which most often affects small and toy breeds and can produce dangerous dehydration within hours.

How long can diarrhea last before I worry?

For a bright, alert adult dog with no blood and no vomiting, mild diarrhoea that is improving within 24 to 48 hours is often manageable at home. Beyond 48 hours without improvement, it needs investigating. The thresholds are much shorter for puppies, seniors and pets with existing conditions — veterinary guidance commonly puts those at 12 to 24 hours — because they dehydrate faster and have less reserve. Cats generally warrant an earlier call than adult dogs for the same reason plus the appetite issue.

Can I give my dog Imodium or Pepto-Bismol?

Not without veterinary approval. These medications can be harmful in pets, can mask a worsening problem, and are contraindicated in some situations — anti-diarrhoeals are a poor idea when an infection or toxin needs to be expelled. Some herding breeds, including Collies and Shelties, carry a genetic sensitivity that makes certain common human drugs dangerous at ordinary doses. Bismuth-containing products also turn stool black, which destroys your ability to spot melena. Ask before giving anything.

What does a bland diet actually mean?

Plain, low-fat, easily digestible food in small frequent portions — commonly boiled skinless chicken breast or lean ground turkey with white rice, with no salt, oil or seasoning. It is a short-term measure rather than a complete diet, so it should not run for weeks without veterinary input. Your vet may instead recommend a prescription gastrointestinal diet, which is formulated to be nutritionally complete and is the better option if the problem is recurring or long-running. Reintroduce the normal food gradually over several days rather than switching back in one meal.
