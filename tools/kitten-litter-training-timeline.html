Straining or frequent trips with little or no urine, especially in a male, can be a blockage and is an emergency.

When Kittens Start Using a Box

Newborn kittens can't eliminate on their own; the mother — or you, if you're bottle feeding — stimulates them after each meal. Kitten Lady introduces a litter box at about 3 weeks and keeps stimulating until the kitten uses the box regularly; PetMD puts the start at around 4 weeks, as weaning begins. Pasadena Humane's chart has kittens discovering the box at 3 weeks and a shallow box placed near them at 4.

Litter: Non-Clumping First

PetMD says kittens under 8–10 weeks should use non-clumping litter, switching to clumping as they get older. Kitten Lady suggests a natural, unscented pellet litter that's less likely to be inhaled or eaten, and waiting until a kitten is at least 2–3 months old and used to the box before trying clumping, because clumping litter is a risk if swallowed.

For adult cats, the FelineVMA says most prefer soft, unscented, clumping litter, and to avoid scented or dusty litter, deodorizers and liners.

The Box

  • Young kittens: an open, shallow box they can climb in and out of easily — veterinarian Sally Foote suggests about 13 × 9 inches (PetMD), and Kitten Lady uses a cardboard tray for kittens under 8 weeks. Kitten Lady advises avoiding tall or covered boxes until the kitten is big enough for an adult box.
  • As they grow: about 1.5 times the cat's length from nose to base of tail (PetMD; FelineVMA). Many commercial boxes are too small; storage bins work well.
  • How many: one per cat plus one, in different places rather than side by side, with at least one on each floor (FelineVMA; PetMD).
  • Where: quiet and private, away from food and water, with no way for a cat to be cornered (FelineVMA). PetMD adds a night-light if the spot is dark.
  • Cleaning: scoop at least once a day; wash the box every one to four weeks with soap and hot water (FelineVMA).

Teaching It

  • Keep kittens of 3–8 weeks in one room, and older kittens within about 10 feet of a box (Kitten Lady).
  • Put the kitten in the box after it eats, drinks or wakes from a nap, until it goes on its own, and reward it straight afterwards (PetMD).
  • Never punish accidents. Clean them with an enzymatic cleaner (PetMD), and if a kitten keeps choosing one spot, put a box there.

Why Isn't My Kitten Using the Litter Box?

  • A medical cause. PetMD lists intestinal parasites and urinary tract infections; the FelineVMA advises an exam and urinalysis first.
  • The setup. Box too small, too tall or covered; scented litter; a noisy, exposed or hard-to-reach spot; or too few boxes.
  • Too much space too soon. A young kitten with the run of the house may not get back to the box in time.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do kittens start using a litter box?

Kitten Lady introduces a box at about 3 weeks, and PetMD around 4 weeks as weaning begins. Before then the mother, or a bottle-feeding caregiver, stimulates the kitten to go after each meal; keep stimulating until the kitten uses the box regularly.

What size litter box does a kitten need?

Start with an open, shallow box the kitten can climb into easily — about 13 × 9 inches, per veterinarian Sally Foote in PetMD. As the kitten grows, aim for about 1.5 times its length from nose to base of tail.

What kind of litter is safe for kittens?

PetMD says kittens under 8–10 weeks should use non-clumping litter. Kitten Lady suggests waiting until a kitten is at least 2–3 months old and used to the box before trying clumping litter, because it's a risk if swallowed. Avoid scented litter.

How many litter boxes do I need for one kitten?

One per cat plus one, according to the Feline Veterinary Medical Association and PetMD — so two for a single kitten, placed in different spots rather than side by side, with one on each floor.

Why is my kitten not using the litter box?

Start with a vet check: PetMD mentions intestinal parasites and urinary tract infections, and the FelineVMA advises an exam and urinalysis for any cat that starts soiling. Then look at the setup — box size and height, litter, location, number of boxes — and how much space the kitten has.
