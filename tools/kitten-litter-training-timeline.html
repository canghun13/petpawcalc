Why Isn't My Kitten Using the Litter Box?

  • Setup issue (check first): box too big or sides too tall to climb, a scent or litter texture they dislike, location too noisy, exposed, or far from where they spend time, or not enough boxes (see the n+1 rule below).
  • Territorial or multi-cat conflict: another cat guarding or ambushing near the box — try adding boxes in separate rooms rather than side by side.
  • Recent change: new litter brand, moved box location, or a household change (new pet, new furniture, moving home) can all cause a temporary lapse.
  • Medical cause: painful elimination from a urinary tract infection or other condition can cause a cat to associate the box itself with pain and avoid it — this needs a vet visit, not more training.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do kittens start using a litter box?

Kittens can typically start using a litter box around 3–4 weeks old, once they begin weaning and their mother stops stimulating them to eliminate. Before that, newborn kittens rely on their mother (or a caregiver, if hand-rearing) to prompt elimination and cannot use a box.

How long does it take to litter train a kitten?

Unlike puppies, most kittens litter train themselves within days if the box is set up correctly — right size, right litter, and an accessible, quiet location — because digging and burying waste is an instinct, not a taught behavior. A kitten adopted at the typical 8–12 week age usually needs no more than a few days to a week to be fully reliable.

What size litter box does a kitten need?

A standard adult litter box is often too large and intimidating for a young kitten. Start with a shallow box around 13×9 inches for kittens under 8 weeks, then size up as they grow — the box should stay roughly 1.5 times your cat's body length at every age, including into adulthood.

What kind of litter is safe for kittens?

Use non-clumping litter for kittens under about 10 weeks old, since young kittens commonly mouth or taste litter and clumping varieties can swell and cause a blockage if ingested. Once you're confident your kitten has stopped tasting the litter, you can transition to clumping if you prefer it.

How many litter boxes do I need for one kitten?

The standard guidance from feline behavior experts is the number of cats in the household plus one — so a single kitten still does best with two boxes, ideally in different locations. This matters more as the household adds cats: three cats calls for four boxes.

Why is my kitten not using the litter box?

Work through setup first: box too large or has tall sides they can't climb, litter type or scent they dislike, box in a noisy or hard-to-reach spot, or not enough boxes for the household. If setup looks right and problems are new or sudden, rule out a medical cause — urinary tract infections and other painful conditions can cause a previously reliable cat to avoid the box, especially if they associate the box itself with the pain.
